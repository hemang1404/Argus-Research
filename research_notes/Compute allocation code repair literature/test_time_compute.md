# Test-time compute allocation, repeated sampling, imperfect verifiers, adaptive stopping (as of 2026-11-11)

Method note and read-depth caveat. Every paper below was fetched from its arXiv abs page (or, for Plan and Budget, a search snippet plus the arXiv abs page) through a summarising fetch tool. That means every entry is ABSTRACT-LEVEL ONLY. I did not read any full paper text, and no table, figure, appendix or experimental-protocol detail was checked. "Limitations" are therefore either stated on the abs page or marked as my inference. Venue labels come from the arXiv abs page comments unless I say otherwise. Where a fetch result said "no venue listed", I report that and do not fill in a venue from memory. Items I could not find are listed under Gaps. arXiv IDs 2604-2608 resolved to real pages in this session (so they are not fabricated), but they are recent and unreplicated.

Read status key: ABS = abstract/abs-page only. SNIP = search-result snippet only.

## Q1. What does each of the ten named papers establish, and where do they conflict?

### Takeaway
The named papers split into three groups. (a) Large Language Monkeys and Snell et al. show that more samples or per-prompt-tuned compute helps when a good verifier exists. (b) Learning How Hard to Think, Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify, DynScaling, CoBa, Belief-Guided Inference Control, Budget Tracker, EET and Plan and Budget allocate compute per input adaptively. They differ in what they allocate (samples, verifier calls, tokens, escalation, tool calls, stopping) and in verifier type. (c) The main conflict is not between these papers but with the imperfect-verifier theory in Q2: coverage-style gains assume an oracle verifier, and cheap proxies cap or reverse them.

### Cited Findings

Per-paper cards (title; year; venue/status; setting; what is allocated; verifier; main result as stated; limitations; read status).

- **Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling** (Brown, Juravsky, Ehrlich, Clark, Le, Ré, Mirhoseini). 2024 (v1 31 Jul 2024, v3 30 Dec 2024). arXiv preprint; the abs page lists no peer-reviewed venue. Setting: coding, math, formal proofs, SWE-bench Lite. Allocates: number of independent samples per problem (uniform). Verifier: automatic verifiers (unit tests, proof checkers) are the focus; majority vote and reward models are the contrast case. Result as stated: coverage (fraction solved by at least one sample) grows roughly log-linearly over four orders of magnitude and is modelled by an exponentiated power law; on SWE-bench Lite with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct, solved issues rise from 15.9% with 1 sample to 56% with 250 samples, above the then single-sample SOTA of 43%; without automatic verifiers, majority voting and reward models plateau beyond several hundred samples. Limitations: uniform allocation, no per-problem adaptivity; the SWE-bench number is coverage under an assumed oracle checker (see Q2); the 56% vs 43% comparison is across different compute budgets (per the summary, not checked in the paper). ABS — [arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)

- **Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters** (Snell, Lee, Xu, Kumar). 2024 (6 Aug 2024). arXiv preprint; the abs page lists no venue (I recall ICLR 2025 but did not verify it in this session). Setting: math reasoning. Allocates: per-prompt choice between searching against a process reward model (PRM) and sequentially revising the response distribution, conditioned on prompt difficulty ("compute-optimal" strategy). Verifier: learned process-based verifier (PRM). Result as stated: compute-optimal allocation improves efficiency more than 4x over a best-of-N baseline; in a FLOPs-matched comparison, where a smaller base model has non-trivial success, test-time compute lets it outperform a 14x larger model. Limitations (abs-page wording): the benefit depends on prompt difficulty, and the 14x claim is restricted to problems where the small model already has non-trivial success. Math only, so it does not cover code execution feedback. ABS — [arXiv 2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)

- **Learning How Hard to Think: Input-Adaptive Allocation of LM Computation** (Damani, Shenfeld, Peng, Bobu, Andreas). 2024 (7 Oct 2024). arXiv preprint; no venue on the abs page (not verified elsewhere). Setting: programming, numerical reasoning, dialog. Allocates: (i) adaptive best-of-k, i.e. how many samples per input, and (ii) routing between a costly accurate decoder and a cheap weak one. Verifier: a reranker / reward model (not an execution oracle, per the abstract). Result as stated: learned allocation policies reduce compute by up to 50% with no loss in quality, or raise quality by up to 10% at fixed budget. Limitations: it predicts the reward distribution from the input before sampling, so it does not use in-run stopping. The abstract does not say whether the verifier is imperfect. ABS — [arXiv 2410.04707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707)

- **Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify: Inference-Time Search with Costly Verification** (Dughmi, Haghifam, Kalayci). 2026 (v1 17 May 2026; v3 7 Nov 2026, described on the abs page as the NeurIPS 2026 camera-ready). Setting: math and competitive programming; a "generative active search" first-positive search problem. Allocates: how many candidates to sample and how many top-ranked ones to send to the expensive verifier, in growing "shells" (algorithm ADAP). Verifier: two-tier. A cheap reward score ranks candidates; an expensive, assumed exact verifier (answer check, hidden-test execution) labels them. Result as stated: with known score distribution, a DP gives the optimal policy; ADAP has expected cost within a constant factor of that optimum if higher scores are no less likely to pass verification (monotonicity); learning-theoretic lower bounds (centered star number) show structural assumptions on the score-label relation are necessary; experiments show gains over fixed and difficulty-adaptive baselines. The abs page gives no numbers or constant. Limitations: the final verifier is assumed exact, so the false-positive problem of Q2 is out of scope; guarantees depend on the monotonic-score assumption (inference from the abstract: this is the assumption that fails if the cheap scorer is miscalibrated). ABS — [arXiv 2605.17609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17609)

- **Plan and Budget: Effective and Efficient Test-Time Scaling on Reasoning Large Language Models** (Lin, Zeng, Zhu, Wang, Shun, Wu, Zhou). First posted 22 May 2025; v3 2 Mar 2026; listed as accepted to ICLR 2026 on the abs page (the ICLR virtual site also lists a poster: [ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008460)). Setting: reasoning benchmarks; the aim is to reduce overthinking. Allocates: token budget across decomposed sub-questions within a single query by estimated complexity (BAM theoretical model, E3 efficiency metric). Verifier: none; the method is not a sampling or verification scheme. Result as stated by the authors: up to 70% accuracy gain, 39% token reduction, 193.8% E3 gain; DS-Qwen-32B reaches E3 0.47 vs 0.50 for DS-LLaMA-70B (search snippet). Limitations: percentages are the authors' own, "up to" best cases, unverified against the full paper; it allocates within-trajectory tokens, not attempts or models. Note the title was extended from the earlier "...on Large Language Model Reasoning" wording to "...Reasoning Large Language Models". ABS — [arXiv 2505.16122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16122)

- **DynScaling: Efficient Verifier-free Inference Scaling via Dynamic and Integrated Sampling** (Wang, Wan, Sun, Chen, Arik). 2025 (19 Jun 2025, v1 only). arXiv preprint; no venue on the abs page (a Google Research publications page also exists: [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/dynscaling-efficient-verifier-free-inference-scaling-via-dynamic-and-integrated-sampling/)). Allocates: samples across queries via a multi-armed-bandit allocator that uses the uncertainty of previously sampled answers; plus an "integrated sampling" step that builds synthetic sequential chains from parallel samples. Verifier: none (verifier-free, relies on self-consistency / LLM-as-judge). Result: the abstract says it "consistently surpasses" verifier-free baselines in performance and cost; no numbers on the abs page. A secondary summary claims ">30% inference cost reduction" but I could not verify that against the paper, so do not cite it. Limitations: no verifier means no false-positive guarantee. Without an oracle, uncertainty is measured by answer disagreement, which is not the same as being correct. ABS — [arXiv 2506.16043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16043)

- **CoBa: Cost-Effective Test-Time Scaling via Compute-Balanced Routing** (Zhou, Ouyang, Zheng, Xiang). 2026 (v1 7 Aug 2026, v2 10 Aug 2026). arXiv preprint, 11 pages. Setting: math (MATH-500, AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023) and procedural symbolic reasoning; 3,129 example-generator evaluations. Allocates: next unit of compute among generate / verify / stop; gather a few candidates, cheap-verify all, send uncertain or high-value ones to a stronger verifier. Verifier: tiered cheap and strong verifiers (self-evaluation weighted voting is a baseline "proxy"). Result as stated: CoBa-Routed-Strong 85.13% macro accuracy, statistically matching a self-evaluation weighted-voting proxy at 85.20% with 49.1% fewer parameter-weighted tokens; within 0.01 macro-accuracy points of best-of-16 majority voting with 58.9% fewer parameter-weighted tokens (small paired-test edge for BoN-16 remains); significant gains over single-sample; a gap to the "pool oracle" remains. Limitations: math/symbolic only, preprint, cost measured in parameter-weighted tokens, not wall-clock or dollars. The 3,129 figure counts example-generator evaluations, not problems. ABS — [arXiv 2608.07424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07424)

- **Belief-Guided Inference Control for Large Language Model Services via Verifiable Observations (VEROIC)** (Yuan, Lin, Chen, Xu, Yang, Ngai). 2026 (30 Apr 2026). Accepted at the KnowFM workshop at ACL 2026 (per abs page), so a workshop paper. Setting: black-box LLM service; per request, keep the cheap default answer or escalate to a stronger, costlier pathway. Allocates: escalation decision under budget (POMDP). Verifier: no external verifier. A belief over latent reliability is built from several quality signals ("verifiable observations"), so it is a proxy. Result: the abstract claims better quality-cost trade-offs, calibration and long-horizon control, with no numbers and no named datasets on the abs page. Limitations: the abs page gives nothing quantitative; the observation channel's false-positive behaviour is not described. ABS — [arXiv 2604.27536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27536)

- **Budget-Aware Tool Use Enables Effective Agent Scaling (Budget Tracker, BATS)** (Liu, Wang, Miao, Hsu, Yan, Chen, Han, Xu, Chen, Jiang, Daruki, Liang, Wang, Pfister, Lee). 21 Nov 2025; v2 17 Aug 2026; abs page lists acceptance at COLM 2026. Setting: web-search agents under tool-call budgets (not coding). Allocates: tool-call/token budget, via a plug-in that exposes remaining budget to the agent, and a planning/verification strategy that adapts to it; a unified token-plus-tool cost metric. Verifier: none external; the "verification" is in-agent self-verification. Result: the abstract states that merely raising the tool-call budget plateaus because agents lack budget awareness, and that budget awareness gives better scaling curves and a better cost-performance Pareto frontier; no numbers on the abs page. Limitations: search agents, not code; the budget-awareness result is about the agent seeing its budget, not about verifier noise. ABS — [arXiv 2511.17006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17006)

- **EET: Experience-Driven Early Termination for Cost-Efficient Software Engineering Agents** (Guo, Xiao, Zhang, Harman, Lou, Liu, Chen). 9 Jan 2026; v2 20 Apr 2026; ACL 2026 Findings (per abs page). Setting: repository-level SWE (SWE-bench Verified, three representative SE agents). Allocates: early termination of unproductive patch generation/selection iterations, guided by structured experience from prior issue-resolution runs. Verifier: no explicit verifier in the abstract; decisions use accumulated experience from earlier runs. Result as stated: total cost reduced 19%-55% (average 32%), resolution rate loss at most 0.2%, early-termination opportunities found for 11% of issues, API calls -21%, input tokens -30%, output tokens -25%; code, prompts and data released. Limitations: it only stops runs, it does not choose models or strategies; it depends on prior-run experience (distribution shift risk is my inference, not stated). This is the only one of the ten that is repository-level coding. ABS — [arXiv 2601.05777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05777)

Where they conflict or do not line up:
- Large Language Monkeys' coverage gains need an oracle verifier; the same paper says reward-model and majority-vote selection plateau — [2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787). Stroebl et al. argue that even unit-test verifiers with limited coverage cap accuracy — [2411.17501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501). So "56% with 250 samples" is a coverage number, not a deployable accuracy number. This is the most important conflict for Argus-style routing.
- Snell et al. find the best strategy depends on difficulty (search vs revision) — [2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314). Dong and Shigida find independent k-shot beats an agentic loop on cost and queries on Codeforces — [2605.08478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08478). These are different domains (math with a PRM vs competitive programming with test feedback), so this is a disagreement about tendency, not a contradiction.
- Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify (exact final verifier, cost-optimal first-positive stop) vs CoBa and VEROIC (proxy/self-evaluation signals) differ on whether stopping is trusted to a noisy signal. Only the former has an optimality guarantee, and it gets it by assuming an exact verifier — [2605.17609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17609), [2608.07424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07424), [2604.27536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27536).
- Plan and Budget reports big gains from token budgeting in a single trajectory ("up to 70%") — [2505.16122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16122), while Budget Tracker reports that merely raising a budget does not help unless the agent is aware of it — [2511.17006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17006); and How Inference Compute Shapes Frontier LLM Evaluation reports that larger token budgets substantially improve performance across several domains — [2606.17930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17930). These are consistent only if "budget awareness / structure" is the moderating variable; the abstracts do not show this directly.
- Related adaptive-allocation work (not on your list, ABS only): Zhai et al., constrained policy optimisation via Lagrangian relaxation, MATH/GSM8K, "up to 12.8% relative accuracy gain" at matched budget, regret bound = imitation error x worst-case gap, preprint — [2604.14853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14853). Zhang et al., OSCA, learns a mixed allocation of samples across configurations (model, temperature, language); stated as accuracy above the best single configuration with 128x less compute on code generation, 25x on 4 reasoning tasks, and 3x less compute on SWE-bench (agentic); no venue on the abs page, v1 only — [2410.22480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22480).

### Inferences
- Treat "56% on SWE-bench Lite" and similar coverage numbers as an upper bound that holds only if the checker is the benchmark's own held-out test set. In Argus terms, coverage is a property of the generator and selection accuracy is a property of the verifier; the two papers on your list that report real numbers for adaptive stopping (CoBa, EET) do so on math or with experience-based heuristics, not with a noisy test oracle.
- Only EET touches repository-level coding among the ten, and it allocates only the stop decision.
- The abstracts for DynScaling, VEROIC, Budget Tracker and Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify carry no numbers. A report writer should not quote effect sizes for those four.

### Gaps
- No full-text reading was done. Any statement about ablations, confidence intervals, seeds, cost accounting, or whether baselines are matched is unknown.
- Venues for Large Language Monkeys, Snell et al., Learning How Hard to Think and DynScaling were not shown on their abs pages. I did not look them up.
- I did not confirm the arXiv numbers for Plan and Budget in the ICLR proceedings beyond the ICLR virtual poster page appearing in search.

## Q2. Coverage vs selection; imperfect verifiers; stopping theory with a noisy verifier (stopping debt, false-positive stops)

### Takeaway
There is real theory: false positives cap resample-until-verified accuracy (Stroebl et al.), accuracy of BoN and rejection sampling is fully determined by the verifier's ROC curve (Dorner et al.), and 2026 papers treat noisy-verifier stopping explicitly (VRR-Stop, always-valid release wrapper). I found no paper that uses the term "stopping debt".

### Cited Findings
- **The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling** (Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan). v1 26 Nov 2024, v3 26 Mar 2026; the abs page lists no venue (a search-result summary says ICLR 2026, which I could not confirm on the abs page, so treat that as unverified). Setting: code (HumanEval, MBPP) with unit-test verifiers. Allocates: number of resamples K. Verifier: unit tests with limited coverage (imperfect). Stated results: resampling cannot reduce the false-positive rate, so accuracy is capped regardless of compute; single-sample accuracy correlates strongly with false-positive rate; a weaker model cannot reach a stronger model's single-sample accuracy by sampling; when false positives carry cost, optimal attempts are often fewer than 10 and the scaling curve bends downward. Limitations: evaluated on HumanEval/MBPP, which are small, simple benchmarks with weak tests; the abs page gives no numeric false-positive rates. ABS — [arXiv 2411.17501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501)
- **ROC-n-reroll: How verifier imperfection affects test-time scaling** (Dorner, Chen, Cruz, Yang). v1 16 Jul 2025, v3 17 Aug 2026; the abs page lists no venue (one search summary says ICLR 2026; not confirmed). 47 pages. Setting: Qwen and LLaMA models on GSM8K and MATH500 (math, not code). Allocates: N for Best-of-N and the acceptance threshold for Rejection Sampling (resample until a score passes). Verifier: imperfect scoring model characterised by its ROC curve. Stated results: instance-level accuracy of both methods is set by the geometry of the verifier ROC curve; rejection sampling beats best-of-N at fixed compute; both converge to the same accuracy as compute goes to infinity; high-compute performance cannot generally be extrapolated from low-compute observations. Limitations: math; the abs page gives no numbers. ABS — [arXiv 2507.12399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12399)
- **Verify, Repair, Repeat, or Stop? Robust Stopping for Noisy Verify-Repair Loops in LLM Agents (VRR-Stop / VRR-Guard)** (Wu, Shen, Yang, Peng, Hu). 20 Jul 2026, preprint under review, cs.AI with cs.SE cross-list. Setting: code generation, math, tool use loops. Allocates: stop vs repair vs commit decisions. Verifier: noisy; four-parameter model separating verifier false acceptance / false rejection from repairer repair / damage rates. Stated results: repair can damage correct plans, and "reported acceptance can rise while true validity falls"; stopping reliability depends on verifier discrimination and decision margin rather than estimation error size; near-zero discrimination can flip the stopping sign, handled by an estimation-free VRR-Guard that replaces the incumbent only on a sufficient verification margin; on a GSM8K stress setting, +60.6 percentage points final true validity over fixed five-round repair at 0.72 average repair rounds. Limitations: the only quantitative result on the abs page is on GSM8K (a stress setting); I have no evidence on code benchmarks from this page. This is the closest match to your "false-positive stop" concept. ABS — [arXiv 2607.17641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17641)
- **When Should an AI Workflow Release? Always-Valid Inference for Black-Box Generate-Verify Systems** (Cho, Sun). 13 May 2026, v1, stat.ML preprint. Setting: iterative generate-evaluate-revise pipelines, MBPP+ coding-agent case study. Allocates: the release/stop decision. Verifier: black-box evaluator score, calibrated against a "reference pool of high-scoring failures", with an e-process accumulating evidence (valid under optional stopping). Stated results: finite-sample control of the probability of releasing on infeasible tasks; the conservative rule still releases on feasible tasks under characterised conditions; on MBPP+ it reduces premature incorrect releases relative to baseline stopping rules (no numbers on the abs page). Limitations: MBPP+ is function-level; "infeasible task" is defined by the pipeline's ability, which needs a reference pool. ABS — [arXiv 2605.12947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12947). A search summary of this paper also says it frames waiting for a high verifier score as analogous to p-hacking under repeated monitoring; I did not see that wording on the abs page, so treat it as a secondary paraphrase.
- Anytime-valid stopping for self-consistency (no verifier): Certified Self-Consistency, Martingale Majority Certificate — [arXiv 2510.17472](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17472v2) (SNIP only, I did not fetch the abs page) and ReASC — [arXiv 2601.02970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02970v1) (SNIP only). I cannot give venue, numbers or reading status beyond a search summary.
- Cost-aware stopping with a cheap score plus an expensive exact verifier is formalised in Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify (constant-factor optimality under monotone scores) — [2605.17609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17609).
- Coverage vs selection from the original repeated-sampling work: coverage keeps rising with samples, but majority voting and reward models plateau beyond several hundred samples without automatic verifiers — [2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787). Test-based selection in code: CodeT scores candidates by dual execution agreement with model-generated tests; HumanEval pass@1 65.8%, +18.8% absolute over code-davinci-002 — [arXiv 2207.10397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10397) (2022, arXiv preprint). AlphaCode relies on large-scale sampling followed by behaviour-based filtering, top 54.3% average ranking in Codeforces contests with over 5,000 participants — [arXiv 2203.07814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07814) (2022; the abs page references a Science DOI, but I did not see a venue statement). The abs pages did not give clustering numbers, so I report none.
- A related evidence line treats the benchmark's own tests as an imperfect verifier: Scaling Agentic Verifier for Competitive Coding — [arXiv 2602.04254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04254) (SNIP only, via search; claim is augmenting test suites with discriminative inputs to cut false positives; unfetched, so numbers unknown).

### Inferences
- Combining Stroebl et al. with VRR-Stop: a resample-until-verified loop on a test oracle has a false-positive floor that is set by the test suite, not by the number of attempts. Any Argus stopping rule that fires on "first pass of the available tests" is exposed to this floor. The decision to stop should depend on how discriminating the verifier is (VRR-Stop's finding), not on a fixed K.
- ROC-n-reroll says the right threshold for rejection sampling is a function of the ROC curve, so a stopping threshold calibrated at low compute may not carry to high compute. That is a reproducibility concern for experiments.
- "Stopping debt" is not an established term in what I found. If Argus uses it, it is Argus's own concept; the nearest published formalisations are "premature incorrect release" (Cho and Sun) and "accepted-but-invalid" (VRR-Stop).

### Gaps
- No search result used the phrase "stopping debt". I did not find a published definition.
- None of the noisy-verifier theory papers that I fetched report repository-level (multi-file, executable test) results on the abs page.
- Venue status of Stroebl et al. and Dorner et al. (ICLR 2026 per search summaries) was not confirmed on the abs pages.
- Full-text content of Certified Self-Consistency, ReASC and the agentic-verifier paper was not read.

## Q3. Sequential refinement with feedback vs independent parallel samples, especially code with execution feedback

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and depends on feedback quality. Self-repair gains are often modest once repair cost is counted (Olausson et al.), but better feedback helps a lot; in competitive programming, independent k-shot beat an agentic loop under fixed budgets (Dong and Shigida); and a 2026 compute-evaluation paper finds repeated submission helps broadly while the benefit of parallel attempts varies by benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet for Code Generation?** (Olausson, Inala, Wang, Gao, Solar-Lezama). v1 16 Jun 2023, v5 2 Feb 2024; the abs page states acceptance to ICLR 2024. Setting: HumanEval and APPS with Code Llama, GPT-3.5, GPT-4. Allocates: number of repair rounds vs sampling. Verifier: test-case execution feedback plus model-written explanation of the error. Stated results: when repair cost is counted, gains are often modest, vary across data subsets, and are sometimes absent; the bottleneck is the model's ability to give useful feedback on its own code; a stronger model supplying the feedback gives substantially larger gains; a small human study found GPT-4 self-repair still well behind human debugging. The abs page has no figures. ABS — [arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896)
- **Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning** (Shinn, Cassano, Berman, Gopinath, Narasimhan, Yao). 2023 (v1 20 Mar 2023, v4 10 Oct 2023), arXiv preprint (no venue on the abs page). Setting: decision-making, coding, language reasoning. Allocates: sequential attempts with episodic memory of verbal reflections on task feedback. Verifier: scalar or free-form feedback from external or simulated sources (for code, test execution). Stated result: 91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80% for GPT-4. Limitation: the abs page does not compare against equal-compute independent sampling, so it does not by itself show sequential beats parallel. ABS — [arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)
- **When Independent Sampling Outperforms Agentic Reasoning** (Dong, Shigida). 8 May 2026, preprint, v1. Setting: 216 Codeforces problems, Divisions 1-3. Allocates: k independent samples vs agent-based reasoning vs budget-partitioned agents, under fixed cost and fixed query budgets. Verifier: not specified on the abs page (the page says accuracy is measured, I assume against the judge, but that is an inference). Stated result: k-shot consistently gets a better accuracy-cost and accuracy-query trade-off across models and difficulties, attributed to lower per-call effectiveness of the agentic approach even with prompt caching; the paper also claims a cost-optimal solver minimises log failure likelihood per dollar. No numbers on the abs page. Limitation: competitive programming, not repository work. ABS — [arXiv 2605.08478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08478)
- **How Inference Compute Shapes Frontier LLM Evaluation** (McFadyen, Jorgensen, Coppock, Wei, Ududec). 16 Jun 2026, v3 16 Jul 2026, preprint. 12 frontier models, 7 benchmarks incl. software engineering. Interventions: larger token budgets, context compaction, repeated submission attempts guided by the model itself or by minimal correctness feedback. Stated results: larger token budgets substantially improve performance in several domains; fixed-budget evaluations can understate newer models; repeated submission broadly helps, while the benefit of larger budgets, external feedback and parallel attempts varies by benchmark; recommends reporting capability as a function of inference compute at matched budgets. No effect sizes on the abs page. ABS — [arXiv 2606.17930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17930)
- DynScaling's integrated sampling converts parallel samples into synthetic sequential chains, which is a hybrid, verifier-free design — [2506.16043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16043). Snell et al. likewise give a difficulty-dependent split between search and sequential revision — [2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314).
- A snippet-only search result (not fetched) reports a SWE-bench Pro / TerminalBench paper where doubling the token budget in serial scaling added only about +0.3 and +1.3 points — the search tool attributed this to a 2026 paper whose arXiv ID and title I did not capture, so I cannot cite it. Listed under Gaps.
- VRR-Stop supplies a mechanism for why sequential loops can hurt: repair can damage already-correct candidates and acceptance can rise while true validity falls — [2607.17641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17641).

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern in these abstracts is that sequential refinement pays when feedback is informative (execution traces, stronger-model critique) and is a poor use of budget when the model must critique itself. Independent sampling plus an exact check is a strong baseline that refinement has to beat at equal cost.
- No abstract I read compared refinement to parallel sampling on repository-level tasks with matched cost, so the SWE-level answer is open.

### Gaps
- Numeric self-repair vs resampling results (the main figures of Olausson et al.) were not on the abs page; need the full paper.
- No fetched source gives an equal-compute comparison of refine vs resample for SWE-bench-style tasks.
- The "doubling the budget adds 0.3 / 1.3 points" finding came from a search snippet only and its paper was not identified.

## Q4. Small model plus more samples vs large model with one sample (code, cost-matched)

### Takeaway
With reliable test-based selection, repeated sampling from smaller code models can beat a larger model at matched budget (gains up to 15% in Hassid et al.). Without tests, ranking-based selection does not match the large model, and with limited-coverage tests the false-positive cap means a weaker model cannot reach a stronger model's single-sample accuracy (Stroebl et al.).

### Cited Findings
- **The Larger the Better? Improved LLM Code-Generation via Budget Reallocation** (Hassid, Remez, Gehring, Schwartz, Adi). v1 31 Mar 2024, v2 25 Jul 2024; COLM 2024 (abs page). Setting: code generation, 5 tasks. Allocates: model size vs number of samples at equal compute or runtime (e.g. one 70B output vs five 13B outputs). Verifier: unit tests when available; ranking-based selection otherwise. Stated results: with unit tests, repeated use of smaller models gives consistent gains of up to 15% across five tasks; without unit tests, ranking-based selection from smaller models falls short of a single large-model output. Limitation: the benchmarks are function-level and models are 2024-era; the abs page gives no cost-accounting detail. ABS — [arXiv 2404.00725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00725)
- **Stroebl et al.** — a weak model's higher false-positive rate means inference scaling cannot let it match a stronger model's single-sample accuracy; the optimal K can be small — [arXiv 2411.17501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501). This directly qualifies Hassid et al.: Hassid's "unit tests" are treated as exact, Stroebl treats them as leaky.
- **Snell et al.** — compute-optimal test-time compute lets a smaller model beat a 14x larger model in FLOPs-matched evaluation on problems where the small model already has non-trivial success (math, PRM) — [arXiv 2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314).
- **OSCA** — mixing model/temperature configurations beats the best single configuration with 128x less compute on code and 3x less on SWE-bench — [arXiv 2410.22480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22480). Limitation (inference): compared against a best single configuration at a given sample count; the abs page does not say how the mix was learned on held-out data.
- Repository-level strong-weak collaboration (Gandhi, Naik, Xie, Rose): weak model handles simpler issues, strong model the hard ones; pipeline and context-based methods are the most cost-efficient; the best strategy matches the strong model's performance at 40% lower cost; preprint, v1 only (26 May 2025), GitHub issue resolution — [arXiv 2505.20182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20182). ABS.
- **SWE-Router** (Son, Yoon, Tang, Wang, Wolf, Bogunovic). 30 Jun 2026, Deep Learning for Code Workshop at ICML 2026. A cheap model explores a few turns, a learned value head on the partial trajectory decides whether to escalate; Bayes-optimality theorem that conditioning on the partial trajectory never hurts; releases a multi-LLM trajectory dataset. The abs page has no numbers. A search summary quotes a Route-AUC of 0.780 vs 0.627 for a non-temporal router on SWE-bench Verified with DeepSeek as the weak model; I did not verify that number on the abs page, so treat it as unverified. ABS — [arXiv 2607.00053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053)
- **Scrouting / SuperScout** (Bhola, Krishnan, NS). 5 Aug 2026, preprint. A 7B repo-scouting searcher writes a handoff (reproduction claims checked in a sandbox), then a router picks one of four frontier fixers; SWE-bench Pro Python slice, 266 tasks, official capped budget tier. Stated results: 159/266 solved vs 158 for the best single model at about one-fifth the cost per solve; searcher overhead under half a cent per task; ablation shows that a no-router variant using the cheapest fixer with the handoff ties the routed system, so the handoff, not routing, drives the result; calibration study N=99, per-fixer effects described as directional only. ABS — [arXiv 2608.04804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04804)
- **Consequence-aware allocation** (He, Wen, Wang, He, Chen, Cui, Wang). Submitted 3 Jun 2026, v2 1 Oct 2026, preprint. SWE-bench Lite. Allocates compute tier per task by predicted consequence of failure. Stated results (qualitative only): consequence carries information beyond difficulty and is predictable before solving; under fixed budget it achieves the best success on high-consequence tasks with overall accuracy "competitive"; a within-model experiment reallocating only inference attempts supports the same advantage. ABS — [arXiv 2606.04402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04402)
- A small Leiden bachelor thesis was returned by search (7B with repeated sampling vs a 32B single generation under a calibrated budget; the 7B won on Python for all groups, not significantly different on C#). It is a student thesis, not peer reviewed, and I did not open it, so do not use it as evidence — [thesis PDF](https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2025-2026-LiJJingbo.pdf) (SNIP only).

### Inferences
- The cost-matched small-vs-large result holds only under an exact selection signal. The two safe operational readings: (1) sample a smaller model heavily only if its tests have low false-positive rates; (2) prefer a cheap-first, escalate-on-fail design (SWE-Router, Scrouting, strong-weak collaboration) that never relies on the small model's self-ranking.
- Scrouting's own ablation (handoff matters, router does not) suggests that, at current benchmark saturation, routing gains over the best single model in accuracy are small and cost savings are the main effect. This is the authors' interpretation, and N is small.

### Gaps
- No cost-matched small-vs-large comparison on repository-level tasks that includes repeated sampling (rather than routing) was found in abstracts.
- Hassid et al. use older models; no 2026 replication was found.
- Absolute numbers for SWE-Router and strong-weak collaboration beyond the 40% figure are not in the abstracts.

## Q5. Papers that formulate a budgeted allocation across models, strategies AND attempts jointly; any on repository-level coding?

### Takeaway
No fetched paper states the exact joint formulation (models x strategies x attempts to maximise probability of a correct solution) as its main problem. The nearest is OSCA (allocating samples across model/temperature/language configurations, with SWE-bench included) and a family of two-way formulations (model x attempt, strategy x attempt). At repository level, only routing/escalation, early termination and tier-allocation papers exist, each covering one or two axes.

### Cited Findings
- **OSCA (Scaling LLM Inference with Optimized Sample Compute Allocation)** — learns how many samples to draw from each configuration (model, temperature, language), i.e. a joint model-and-attempt allocation; also evaluated on agentic SWE-bench with 3x less compute than the default configuration (author-stated). Not repository-level specific, and strategies in the sense of agent scaffolds are not an axis in the abstract. 2024, v1, no venue on abs page — [arXiv 2410.22480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22480). ABS.
- **Adaptive Test-Time Compute Allocation via Constrained Policy Optimization** (Zhai et al.) — maximise expected accuracy under an average compute budget, choosing an action per input (action set includes model and sampling choices per the models listed: DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen2.5-7B; the abs page does not describe the action space precisely); regret bound; math only; preprint, 16 Apr 2026 — [arXiv 2604.14853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14853). ABS.
- **CoBa** — frames the next unit of compute as generate vs verify vs stop and routes across generator and verifier tiers; math/symbolic only — [arXiv 2608.07424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07424). ABS.
- **Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify** — jointly allocates samples and verifier calls with a cost-optimality guarantee; one generator; math and competitive programming — [arXiv 2605.17609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17609). ABS.
- **Learning How Hard to Think** — jointly covers the sample-count decision and the decoder-routing decision, but as two separate procedures — [arXiv 2410.04707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707). ABS.
- **Compute Allocation for Self-Evolving LLMs: From Depth-Breadth to Multi-Armed Bandits (BaSE)** (Xing et al.). 28 May 2026, v3 12 Sep 2026, preprint. Allocates LLM calls between depth and breadth across parallel trajectories using a bandit; five models, three tasks; mean fitness +12.3% over the strongest island-protocol baseline across 8 (model, task) cells, with the largest gains in high-variance settings. It is for evolutionary search (fitness), not pass/fail correctness — [arXiv 2605.29268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29268). ABS.
- Repository-level, single or double axis: EET (stop only) — [2601.05777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05777); SWE-Router (model escalation with partial trajectory) — [2607.00053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053); Scrouting (which fixer; handoff) — [2608.04804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04804); strong-weak collaboration — [2505.20182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20182); consequence-aware tier allocation — [2606.04402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04402); independent k-shot vs agentic vs budget-partitioned agents on Codeforces (not repository-level) — [2605.08478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08478).
