## 1. Degenerate repetition, looping and overthinking in reasoning LLMs

### Takeaway
Looping under greedy/low-temperature decoding is common and measured: 37-76% of AIME responses for R1-distilled Qwen at T=0, 81% of one safety-prompt set for gpt-oss-20b under greedy, and the Qwen3 model card and gpt-oss README both say not to use greedy (Qwen3: T=0.6/top-p 0.95/top-k 20; gpt-oss: T=1.0/top-p 1.0). Loops are concentrated on harder problems for most models (ICML 2026 paper), but I found no published work that uses loop detection itself as a compute-allocation signal; the nearest are restart-on-trap (TAAR), adaptive stopping by difficulty (REFRAIN, snippet-only), and CUSUM early loop prediction.

### Cited Findings

**Prevalence, trigger, and why (peer-reviewed)**
- "Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?" (Pipis, Garg, Kontonis, Shrivastava, Krishnamurthy, Papailiopoulos; arXiv 2512.12895, Dec 2025; published ICML 2026, PMLR v306 pp. 98934-98964). Read: venue page + arXiv HTML (first ~100k of ~141k chars; appendix tail not read). Loop = any n-gram repeated >=k times (reasoning models n=30, k=20; instruct k=10); AIME24+25, 20 samples/problem/temperature, T in 0..1.0 step 0.2; GPQA spot check found same trends. At greedy T=0, fraction of responses looping: R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B 0.76, 7B 0.49, 32B 0.37; OpenThinker3 1.5B 0.30, 7B 0.10; QwQ-32B (teacher) 0.04; R1-Distill-Llama-8B 0.54; Phi-4-reasoning 0.43; Phi-4-reasoning-plus 0.45. Looping falls as temperature rises but "does not fix the underlying learning errors" (outputs stay longer than needed; toy task at T=1: 105.5 tokens vs 24.8 for a perfect learner). Mechanisms: students loop far more than teachers; a hard-to-learn progress step vs an easy cyclic step makes greedy pick the cycle (risk aversion); Transformer bias toward temporally correlated errors; repetition becomes more confident as it continues. Temperature called a stopgap; fixes proposed are training-time (hint augmentation at high-loss positions, margin training in toy setting only). RL post-training had limited effect on looping in the Phi-4 family. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12895v1), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/pipis26a.html)
- Concentration on hard prompts: same paper, on the 20 hardest vs 20 easiest AIME problems looping at T=0 was significantly higher on the hard set for most models; Phi-4-reasoning is an exception (loops more on easy problems, often restating its final answer repeatedly). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12895v1)
- "Circular Reasoning: Understanding Self-Reinforcing Loops in Large Reasoning Models" (Duan, Pang, et al., ICT-CAS; arXiv 2601.05693, 9 Jan 2026; preprint, no venue found). Read: HTML first ~100k of ~158k chars (intervention appendix not read). LoopBench; numerical loop = repeat unit x count >500 tokens; statement loop = sentence recurring >3 times. Open models, balanced setting: DS-Qwen-7B 62.00% combined (17.14% numerical, 44.86% statement); Phi-4-reasoning-plus 29.90% numerical; closed models lower but non-zero (gemini-2.5-pro 7.43%, claude-opus-4-1 6.71%); instruct models ~0% statement loops. On AIME25 and SuperGPQA most models usually <~10%. Temperature: DS-Qwen-7B combined rate 92.07% (T=0.1), 62.00% (T=0.6), 18.71% (T=1.0); numerical loops persist at T=1.0. No significant correlation of repetition with model scale or distillation status; the paper does NOT directly test problem difficulty. Models evaluated include gpt-oss-20B, Qwen3 8B/14B/32B, QwQ-32B. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05693)
- "Quant Fever, Reasoning Blackholes, Schrodinger's Compliance, and More: Probing GPT-OSS-20B" (arXiv 2509.23882, Sept 2025; preprint). Read: HTML section 3.2 only. Under greedy decoding, 81% (162/200) of JBB-Bench prompts (jailbreak/safety prompts, not code) fell into "reasoning blackholes" and the model could not recover; top-1 token probability approaches ~100% after ~100 tokens; localized attention at layers 12/23; larger batch sizes make blackholes more likely; prompts differing only by date in the Harmony format triggered them at different rates. Reports only the greedy rate (no sampled rate), proposes no fixes. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23882)

**Recommended sampling / documented fixes**
- Qwen3 model card (HF Qwen/Qwen3-32B, read in full for the best-practices section): thinking mode T=0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, min-p 0; non-thinking T=0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, min-p 0; "DO NOT use greedy decoding" (degradation and endless repetitions); presence_penalty 0-2 can reduce endless repetition but higher values may cause language mixing and slightly lower performance; output length 32,768 tokens typical, 38,912 for hard math/competition programming. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-32B)
- gpt-oss: official GitHub README (primary) says "We recommend sampling with temperature=1.0 and top_p=1.0"; reasoning effort low/medium/high via system prompt ("Reasoning: high"). No guidance on repetition penalties. — [openai/gpt-oss README](https://github.com/openai/gpt-oss). Unsloth's guide repeats temperature=1.0, top_p=1.0, top_k=0 (or experiment with 100) and does not mention loops (secondary) — [Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gpt-oss-how-to-run-and-fine-tune). The HF gpt-oss-20b generation_config.json contains only do_sample:true and token ids (no temperature/top_p) — [HF file](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b/raw/main/generation_config.json). The HF model card page gives no sampling values and no loop statements — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b).
- Practitioner reports of gpt-oss looping (blog post on gpt-oss:20b in an agent setup; HF forum thread on gpt-oss-120b endless output after vLLM v0.11.x upgrade) are anecdotal, snippet-only, and I did not locate a specific tracker issue — [blog](https://blog.tymscar.com/posts/gptoss/), [HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/never-ending-inference-until-it-reaches-max-context-length/173351)

**Overthinking in agentic coding**
- "The Danger of Overthinking: Examining the Reasoning-Action Dilemma in Agentic Tasks" (Cuadron et al., 16 authors incl. Stoica, Neubig, Gonzalez; arXiv 2502.08235, 12 Feb 2025; venue not stated on the page). Abstract-only. SWE-bench Verified, 4,018 trajectories; three patterns: Analysis Paralysis, Rogue Actions, Premature Disengagement; higher overthinking score correlates with lower performance; reasoning models overthink more than non-reasoning; selecting the lower-overthinking solution improves performance "by almost 30%" and cuts compute 43% (abstract wording). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08235)
- "The Limits of Long-Context Reasoning in Automated Bug Fixing" (arXiv 2602.16069). Snippet-only: reports failed examples have much longer average token counts than passed ones (about 15.5k vs 8.5k for DeepSeek R1). Not fetched; verify before use. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16069v1)

**Budget / early-exit / thinking-budget work**
- s1: Simple test-time scaling (Muennighoff et al.; arXiv 2501.19393, 31 Jan 2025, v3 1 Mar 2025; venue not stated). Abstract-only. Budget forcing = forcibly terminate thinking or append "Wait"; Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct fine-tuned on s1K (1,000 examples); AIME24 50% -> 57% with scaling via budget forcing; beats o1-preview on MATH/AIME24 by up to 27%. A later critique ("It's Not That Simple") was seen only in a listing snippet and is unverified. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393)
- Qwen3 Technical Report (An Yang et al.; arXiv 2505.09388, 14 May 2025). Abstract-only: thinking mode + non-thinking mode, and a "thinking budget mechanism, allowing users to allocate computational resources adaptively during inference" (latency vs performance by task difficulty). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)
- Stop Overthinking: A Survey on Efficient Reasoning for LLMs (Sui et al.; arXiv 2503.16419; v4 Aug 2025; TMLR 2025). Abstract-only. Taxonomy: model-based, reasoning-output-based (dynamic reduction at inference), input-prompt-based (difficulty / length control). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16419)
- ThinkLess (arXiv 2505.15684, May 2025; v2 23 May 2025): training-free early termination by inserting a reasoning-terminator token plus a small format-restoring instruction; targets R1-distilled models; reports comparable accuracy with large decoding-time/memory savings (no numbers captured). Snippet-only. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15684v2)
- ESTAR (arXiv 2602.10004), CoRE (arXiv 2507.06087; hidden-state trajectories for early exit), Speculative Probing (arXiv 2608.28099; benchmark for detecting repetitive loops in think traces), activation probes (arXiv 2607.21433; reported to beat token-entropy/repetition-statistic baselines): all snippet-only, not fetched. — [ESTAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10004v1), [CoRE](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06087v1), [Speculative Probing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28099)
- Open-source stream loop detectors (loop-guard, pi-antiloop, pi-loop-police) use consecutive-line matching / n-gram similarity / sliding-window density; unreviewed, performance unverified (snippet-only) — [loop-guard README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/isr4el-silv4/loop-guard@master/README.md)

**Loop detection / early termination as a compute-allocation signal (closest work)**
- Circular Reasoning paper: CUSUM change-point detector on semantic repetition predicts statement loops before textual repetition. Early Detection Rate 0.64-0.76 (DS-Qwen-14B 0.76, Qwen3-8B 0.64), False Positive Rate 0.24-0.34 (DS-Qwen-14B 0.28), ~1,300-1,870 tokens before loop onset; without persistence EDR is higher (0.84) but FPR rises (0.40; up to 0.62 for Phi-4-reasoning). Calibrated on 50 non-loop instances per model; run with greedy decoding. Intervention results not read. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05693)
- "Thinking Traps in Long Chain-of-Thought: A Measurable Study and Trap-Aware Adaptive Restart" (TAAR; Chen et al.; arXiv 2601.11940, 17 Jan 2026; ACL Findings 2026 per search listing, ACL Anthology URL 404 on direct fetch). Abstract-only. 89% of failures on a curated DAPO-MATH subset show "thinking traps" (prefix-dominant deadlocks); a diagnostic policy truncates before the trap and restarts decoding, with stronger perturbation (higher-temperature resampling) for severe cases; improves AIME24/25, GPQA-Diamond, HMMT25, BRUMO25 without fine-tuning. Trigger is a wrong early commitment, not literal token repetition. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11940)
- REFRAIN (training-free; discriminator for reflection-to-redundant-repetition transition plus SW-UCB bandit thresholds set by problem difficulty). Snippet-only; I did not retrieve its arXiv ID. — see search result in [TAAR listing](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.1930/)
- "It's the Problem, Not the Path: Budget and Difficulty Confounds in LLM Reasoning Trajectories" (arXiv 2609.03436): snippet-only; uses an empty-prefix restart baseline at matched total token budget, a useful control for any restart-based allocation claim. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03436)
- Danger of Overthinking (above) is the closest agentic-coding example of a behavioral signal used to choose among low-effort samples (30% gain / 43% cost reduction claims, abstract-only).

### Inferences
- Because looping is far more likely at T=0 and on harder problems (Pipis), a deterministic temperature-0 eval of a reasoning model will conflate "hard task" with "greedy pathology": a loop at T=0 on gpt-oss/Qwen3 is partly a decoding artifact, so the vendor-recommended sampling settings should be the baseline before treating loops as a difficulty signal.
- A loop/abort event is a plausible but untested difficulty proxy; the only direct evidence of difficulty-correlation is Pipis on AIME (math, not code), with the Phi-4 exception showing it is not universal. Circular Reasoning explicitly did not test difficulty.
- Any claim that "restart after loop recovers X% of tasks" needs a matched-budget fresh-sample control (cf. 2609.03436).

### Gaps
- No evidence found on loop rates for gpt-oss or Qwen3 on code-repair prompts specifically; the gpt-oss number (81%) is on jailbreak/safety prompts under greedy.
- No source fetched for DRY / repetition-penalty effectiveness on reasoning models; only Qwen3's presence-penalty note and a hosting provider's generic repetition_penalty ~1.1 tip (snippet-only, not gpt-oss-specific).
- No published work found that treats loop detection itself as an explicit compute-allocation / routing signal.
- OpenAI's official statement on why gpt-oss uses T=1.0 / any loop guidance: none found (model card not fetched).
- Venue for Cuadron et al. not confirmed; Circular Reasoning venue not found.

## 2. Edit formats and patch-application robustness

### Takeaway
Published evidence agrees that format choice matters and that SEARCH/REPLACE is best for stronger models while small models stay poor under any format (Diff-XYZ), that Aider's flexible patching cut edit errors ~9x for GPT-4 Turbo with udiff (no absolute counts given), and that whitespace-normalized matching recovers a large share of perturbed edits at essentially zero silent-misapply risk while fuzzy matching adds more recovery at a real wrong-location risk. No source quantifies "share of resolved tasks attributable to tolerance"; the user's 6/~38 (whitespace) vs ~1 (true fuzzy) is directionally consistent with Aider's own code (leading-whitespace tolerance active, fuzzy matcher dead code) but not directly comparable.

### Cited Findings

**Aider benchmarks and edit formats**
- Aider "Unified diffs make GPT-4 Turbo 3X less lazy" (aider.chat/docs/unified-diffs.html; blog 2023). Read: page. SEARCH/REPLACE-style baseline for GPT-4 Turbo 20% with lazy comments on 12 tasks; unified diff 61% with lazy comments on 4 tasks (3x reduction). gpt-4-0613: 26% -> 59% (8k context caps at 72%). Disabling flexible patching: "9X increase in editing errors" on the original Exercism benchmark; disabling high-level diff prompting: editing errors up 30-50%. No per-strategy ablation. Flexible-patching strategies: hunk normalization, recovering unmarked additions, relative leading whitespace, hunk splitting, context-window variation. — [Aider unified diffs](https://aider.chat/docs/unified-diffs.html)
- Aider edit-formats doc: whole, diff (SEARCH/REPLACE), diff-fenced (Gemini; "often fail to conform to the fencing approach"), udiff (GPT-4 Turbo; reduces lazy elision), editor-diff/editor-whole (architect mode). No failure rates given. — [Aider edit formats](https://aider.chat/docs/more/edit-formats.html)
- Aider's SEARCH/REPLACE applier (editblock_coder.py, main branch; I verified the dead-code point directly with curl+grep). Cascade: exact match; `replace_part_with_missing_leading_whitespace` (comment: "GPT often messes up leading whitespace": strips common indent from SEARCH and REPLACE, finds window matching modulo a consistent indent offset, re-adds offset to REPLACE); retry dropping a spurious leading blank line (issue #25); `...` elision handling (each segment must occur exactly once); filename/fence stripping; if edit fails against named file, tries other files in chat. The fuzzy fallback `replace_closest_edit_distance` (intended similarity 0.8, length window +/-10%) is unreachable because a bare `return` precedes it. Failed edits are reported with "Did you mean" hints (similarity cutoff 0.6, hint only). — [editblock_coder.py](https://github.com/Aider-AI/aider/blob/main/aider/coders/editblock_coder.py)
- Aider polyglot leaderboard data (225 test cases; fields pass_rate_2, percent_cases_well_formed): gpt-oss-120b (high), diff: 41.8% pass_rate_2, 79.1% well-formed (77 malformed responses, 47 cases with malformed); Qwen3 32B diff: 40.0%, 83.6% (50 malformed, 37 cases); Qwen3 235B A22B (no think) diff: 59.6%, 92.9%; QwQ-32B diff: 20.9%, 67.6% (143 malformed, 73 cases); Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct (hyperbolic) diff: 8.0%, 71.6% (148 malformed, 64 cases); same model whole: 16.4%, 99.6%; R1 (0528) 71.4%, 94.6%; R1 (Jan 20) 56.9%, 96.9%; gpt-4.1-nano whole: 8.9%, 94.2%; Gemma 3 27B whole: 4.9%, 100%. Lowest well-formed overall: chatgpt-4o-latest 64.4%, QwQ-32B 67.6%, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B (hyperbolic) 71.6%, Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking 77.8%, gpt-oss-120b (high) 79.1%. gpt-oss-20b is not on the page. — [polyglot_leaderboard.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Aider-AI/aider/main/aider/website/_data/polyglot_leaderboard.yml)
- Aider's older benchmark page (Exercism, 133 exercises) gives GPT-3.5 whole 46% (Feb) vs 39% (June); diff 30% -> ~19%; notes GPT-3.5 used diff "pathologically" by putting the entire original file in the ORIGINAL block. — [Aider benchmarks](https://aider.chat/docs/benchmarks.html)

**Diff-XYZ and other academic format studies**
- Diff-XYZ: A Benchmark for Evaluating Diff Understanding (Glukhov, Conti, Bogomolov, Golubev, Bezzubov; JetBrains Research; arXiv 2510.12487, v1 14 Oct 2025, v2 17 Nov 2025; no venue found). Read: abstract + HTML v2 tables. 1,000 real edits from CommitPackFT; tasks apply / anti-apply / diff generation; formats udiff, udiff-h, udiff-l, search-replace. "Search-replace is the most effective representation overall, especially for larger models." EM (apply / anti-apply / diff-gen): GPT-4o udiff 0.86/0.85/0.41 vs search-replace 0.94/0.94/0.73; GPT-4.1 0.90/0.88/0.81 vs 0.96/0.93/0.95; Claude 4 Sonnet 0.95/0.82/0.82 vs 0.97/0.87/0.94; Gemini 2.5 Flash 0.90/0.34/0.72 vs 0.95/0.85/0.88; GPT-4.1-nano udiff 0.37/0.02/0.50 vs search-replace 0.54/0.01/0.07; Qwen2.5-Coder 32B 0.84/0.87/0.23 vs 0.92/0.85/0.68; 7B 0.58/0.65/0.06 vs 0.75/0.71/0.28; 3B 0.38/0.17/0.01 vs 0.41/0.29/0.14; 1.5B 0.16/0.04/0.00 vs 0.22/0.08/0.20; 0.5B ~0 on everything. Qwen diff-generation parsing rate reaches 0.99 only at 32B; no open model matches proprietary on diff generation. udiff-l is weakest on diff generation. Note: this measures apply/generate competence in isolation, not repo-level repair. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12487), [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12487v2)
- "Look Before You Leap: Pre-Action Verification for LLM Agents" (Althoubi; arXiv 2609.11957; page says submitted 9 Aug 2026, but the ID prefix 2609 implies Sept 2026: date/ID inconsistency; preprint). Read: abstract + HTML table. 640 edits over 224 real Python files (23,040 trials), synthetic perturbations (no small models; one unspecified Claude model for a 9-edit probe). Clean success 1.000 for all content-anchored formats. Stress success / stress silent-misapply / multi-hunk silent: SR-exact 0.176 / 0.000 / 0.000; SR-whitespace-normalized 0.571 / 0.000 / 0.000; SR-fuzzy 0.977 / 0.023 / 0.393; unified diff 0.375-0.536 / 0.000 / 0.000; line-range 0.000 / 0.991 / 1.000. Anchor-and-verify applier: one silent misapplication in 8,320 trials. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11957), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11957)
- Copy-as-Decode (Liu; arXiv 2604.18170, 20 Apr 2026). WITHDRAWN: v2 (24 May 2026) is withdrawn by the authors "following internal review regarding authorship and contribution agreements." Abstract-only. Reported pooled exact match falls from 100% to 15.48% under off-by-one noise. Snippet-level claims about SEARCH/REPLACE round-trip 0.81-0.94 and anchor ambiguity were NOT confirmed on the abstract page. Do not rely on it. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18170)
- SWE-Edit: Rethinking Code Editing for Efficient SWE-Agent (Zhang et al.; arXiv 2604.26102, 28 Apr 2026, v2 26 May 2026; preprint). Abstract-only. Splits Viewer/Editor subagents; +2.1 pp SWE-bench Verified, -17.9% cost; GRPO-trained Qwen3-8B editor with adaptive find-replace vs whole-file rewrite policy improves edit success by 12.5 pp and reaches parity with GPT-5-nano downstream. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26102)
- Line-Anchored Feedback Cuts Token Costs... (Lamberti; arXiv 2607.12713, 14 Jul 2026). Abstract-only; local models' SEARCH/REPLACE compliance details not retrieved (snippet claimed some local models emitted full scripts or malformed hybrids instead of the block format: unverified). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12713)

**Original SWE-bench evidence on patch format**
- SWE-bench (Jimenez et al.; arXiv 2310.06770; published at ICLR 2024 per my background knowledge, not verified in this session). Read: HTML first ~100k of ~190k chars. Models "still struggle with generating well-formatted patch files." Table 5 % of patches that apply (BM25, full SWE-bench): Claude 3 Opus 46.56, Claude 2 43.07, SWE-Llama 7b 51.74, SWE-Llama 13b 53.62, GPT-4-turbo 26.90, ChatGPT-3.5 26.33. A Table 14 "patch fix" step (share of applied patches that needed repair): ChatGPT-3.5 BM25-13k 604 applied / 363 fixed (60.1%); Claude 2 BM25-13k 988 / 302 (30.57%); Claude 2 oracle 1,441 / 360 (24.98%). Caution: my reading of "fixed" is the fetch tool's interpretation of the table; verify column semantics in the paper before citing. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06770v3)

**Tool-level tolerance (docs/source)**
- OpenAI Codex `apply_patch` (codex-rs/apply-patch/src/seek_sequence.rs): context lines matched in four passes of decreasing strictness: exact, trailing-whitespace stripped (`trim_end`), both-sides `trim`, then trim plus Unicode punctuation normalisation (dashes, curly quotes, special spaces to ASCII). No fuzzy similarity matching. — [seek_sequence.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/codex/main/codex-rs/apply-patch/src/seek_sequence.rs)
- Anthropic text editor tool `str_replace`: `old_str` "must match exactly, including whitespace and indentation"; the application must ensure exactly one match, else return an error (docs example "Error: No match found for replacement"). — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/text-editor-tool)
- `git apply`: `-C<n>` requires at least n lines of context to match before/after each change (fewer allowed at edges; default no context ignored); `--unidiff-zero` bypasses the at-least-one-context-line check for `--unified=0` diffs (context-free patches "discouraged"); `--ignore-space-change/--ignore-whitespace` ignores whitespace changes in context lines; `--recount` ignores hunk-header line counts; `--inaccurate-eof` works around missing-newline bugs; `--whitespace=<fix|warn|error...>`. Note `-C` is a context-strictness limit, not fuzz, and git apply has no fuzzy line-offset search beyond exact context with offsets. — [git-apply docs](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-apply)
- Cline blog "Improving diff edits by 10%": new "order-invariant multi-diff apply" (handles out-of-order blocks), multiple diff formats per model family (Anthropic `---`/`+++`; Gemini and xAI `>>>`/`<<<`), model-specific prompts; `diffEditSuccess` improved "over 10% on average", Claude 3.5 Sonnet nearly 25%, GPT-4.1 models over 21%, Claude Opus 4 almost 15%. Vendor first-party telemetry; no per-model absolute rates; blog does not mention fuzzy/line-trim fallbacks. — [Cline blog](https://cline.bot/blog/improving-diff-edits-by-10)
- Morph (competing vendor) pages cite 35-89% failure for text-matching edits: marketing, unverified methodology, snippet-only; exclude.

### Comparing with the user's finding (~6 of ~38 resolved from whitespace/indent tolerance, ~1 from true fuzzy)
- Direction agrees with Aider: leading-whitespace tolerance is an explicitly engineered, active fallback ("GPT often messes up leading whitespace") while the similarity-based fuzzy fallback is dead code in current Aider main, i.e. Aider itself ships without active fuzzy matching.
- Direction agrees with 2609.11957: on perturbed edits whitespace normalization lifts success from 0.176 to 0.571 with zero silent misapply, while fuzzy lifts to 0.977 but with 2.3% single-hunk and 39.3% multi-hunk silent misapply. So a small tail gain from "true fuzzy" at nonzero wrong-location risk is the expected pattern. Magnitude not comparable: those are synthetic perturbation success rates over edits, not share of resolved tasks.
- Aider's only quantified tolerance effect is the 9x edit-error increase when all flexible patching is disabled (GPT-4 Turbo, udiff, Exercism); that is share-of-errors, not share-of-resolved-tasks, and covers multiple mechanisms jointly.
- 6/38 is about 16% of resolved tasks (1/38 about 2.6%); with such small counts the binomial uncertainty is wide (my arithmetic, not from a source).

### Inferences
- Small models are bottlenecked by both format adherence and apply/generate competence; Diff-XYZ shows search-replace helps down to ~1.5B-7B for apply but not reliably for generation, and Aider's leaderboard shows reasoning models (QwQ-32B 67.6%, gpt-oss-120b 79.1%) with lower well-formed rates under diff format than R1 (94.6-96.9%), though a non-reasoning model (Qwen2.5-Coder-32B via one provider) also scored 71.6%, so reasoning vs non-reasoning is not isolated and provider/serving effects confound.
- Whitespace/indent normalization is the high-yield, low-risk tolerance tier; fuzzy matching should be treated as a separate, risky tier that needs anchor-and-verify (unique-match) safeguards.

### Gaps
- No published number found for the fraction of otherwise-lost patches (or resolved tasks) recovered by tolerant application in a repo-level setting; Aider gives only the 9x error ratio. Aider's blog gives no absolute counts for the flexible-patching ablation.
- No Aider polyglot entry for gpt-oss-20b; no per-model-size diff-application failure rates beyond Diff-XYZ's (single-file, non-agentic) results and Aider's well-formed percentages.
- SWE-bench Table 14 semantics unverified; the Copy-as-Decode paper is withdrawn; 2609.11957 has a date/ID inconsistency.
- Codex apply_patch format spec page (tool instructions .md) returned 404; behaviour taken from source only.

## 3. Fault localization (issue-only vs test/traceback-driven)

### Takeaway
Issue-only file-level localization on SWE-bench Lite: BM25 38.69 / 51.82 / 61.68 (Acc@1/3/5); embeddings up to 52.55 / 77.74 / 84.67 (CodeRankEmbed) and 72.63 / 91.24 / 94.16 (SweRankEmbed-Large); agents 72.63-77.74 Acc@1 and 79.56-94.16 Acc@5; Agentless's own hierarchical file step contains the ground-truth file in 78.67% (prompting), 70.33% (embedding), 81.67% (combined). The one clean published test-driven datapoint I found is AutoCodeRover's spectrum-based FL (needs a test suite): 19% -> 22% resolved (57 -> 66 of 300), about +$0.04 and +55 s per task. I found no direct head-to-head of issue-only vs failing-test+traceback localization recall.

### Inferences
- For strong models/agents file-level issue-only localization on SWE-bench Lite is already ~80-94% by Acc@5, so file recall is rarely the binding constraint; RGFL shows only 13% of unresolved Verified failures had the wrong file, and 2605.12270 reports localization as the lowest-failure stage. For small models this is unverified.
- Because Lite issues give a file-level location in about half of cases (Agentless manual classification), BM25 Acc@5 ~62% is plausibly driven by identifiers in the issue text; this is my inference, not a stated result.
- Test-driven signals help a modest amount in the one clean published ablation (+3 pp for ACR SBFL), and carry a stack-trace misleading failure mode in the taxonomy literature; so "failing test with traceback" should not be assumed to dominate issue-only retrieval.

### Gaps
- No published direct comparison of issue-only vs failing-test+traceback file localization recall (recall@1/@5) found; IssueExec is closest but abstract-only and unnamed baselines. A search for traceback-specific SWE-bench ablations returned nothing direct.
- No localization recall numbers for small (<=20B) reasoning models in an Agentless-style pipeline found.
- Agentless does not report top-k recall; top-k numbers come from SweRank/LocAgent tables (different pipelines/models).

## 4. Published taxonomies of failure modes for repository-level repair (placing a new taxonomy)

### Takeaway
There are at least two manual-annotation SWE-bench Verified taxonomies (Liu et al. 2025: 3 phases/9 categories/25 subcategories from 150 failures; Jiang et al. 2026: five stages from 243 failed attempts across 3 frontier models), one behavioral overthinking taxonomy (Cuadron et al.), plus a multi-agent taxonomy (MAST) and a cross-benchmark synthesis (snippet-only). They are built from large frontier-model trajectories, so a taxonomy for small/reasoning models adds repetition/degeneration and edit-application failures that those mostly do not isolate.

### Cited Findings
- "An Empirical Study on Failures in Automated Issue Solving" (arXiv 2509.13941, Sept 2025; authors not captured by the fetch; preprint). Read: HTML. Tools (all Claude 3.5 Sonnet backbone): OpenHands-CodeAct-2.1 53.0% (265/500), Agentless-1.5 50.8% (254/500), Tools+Claude 49.0% (245/500) on SWE-bench Verified. Taxonomy from 150 sampled failures (342 failure instances; Cohen's kappa 0.72-0.77): Phase A Localization (A1 Issue Misleading; A2 Superficial Information Matching: keywords, referred code, error stack trace); Phase B Repair (B1 Fix Strategy Defects: specific-case overfitting, evasive repair, redundant erroneous implementation; B2 Implementation Details Defects: logic, data processing, insufficient domain knowledge; B3 Incomplete Repair: ignoring explicit/semantic dependencies; B4 Issue Interference); Phase C Iteration & Validation (C1 Reproduction/Verification Failure; C2 Iteration Anomalies: non-progressive iteration, blind strategy switching, validation retreat; C3 Context Amnesia). Findings: localization error in 51.3% of Agentless failures; iteration/validation errors in 49.1% (OpenHands) and 51.7% (Tools Claude); root causes ~65% flawed reasoning, ~25% knowledge deficiency, ~10% environmental friction; failed issues used ~3.5x more interaction steps; multi-file success 25.3% / 18.3% / 11.3% vs 55-58% single-file; hard tasks 13-20%. Expert-Executor supervisor resolved 24 of 108 previously failed issues (22.2%) vs 7 for a baseline. Not all 25 subcategories were listed on the page. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13941v1)
- "Characterizing the Failure Modes of LLMs in Resolving Real-World GitHub Issues" (Jiang, Huang, Wang, Chen, Liu, Briand; arXiv 2605.12270, 12 May 2026; preprint). Abstract-only. Claude 4.5 Sonnet, Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5 on SWE-bench Verified; 243 failed of 900 trials manually analysed; five-stage taxonomy; strategy formulation and logic synthesis is the most error-prone stage for all models, problem understanding next, localization lowest; robustness and operational cost vary significantly; harnesses sometimes reject correct patches due to superficial differences or hidden constraints. Names of other two stages and percentages not captured. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12270)
- The Danger of Overthinking (Cuadron et al., 2025): Analysis Paralysis, Rogue Actions, Premature Disengagement over 4,018 SWE-bench Verified trajectories (abstract-only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08235)
- RGFL's stage-wise error attribution on unresolved Verified (wrong file 13%, element 53%, line 84%; 21 of 209 had all localization correct yet failed). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18044)
- SWE-bench patch-format difficulty (models "struggle with generating well-formatted patch files") — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06770v3)
- Problem-quality confounds: Agentless's manual classification of Lite (4.3% gold patch in description; 5.0% misleading; 10.0% insufficient information) and Lite-S exclusion — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489)
- MAST (arXiv 2503.13657): first structured failure taxonomy for multi-agent systems (not SWE-bench specific). Snippet-only. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657.pdf)
- "Beyond the Leaderboard: A Synthesis of Tool-Use, Planning, and Reasoning Failures in LLM Agents" (arXiv 2607.05775, 7 Jul 2026): cross-benchmark taxonomy, six clusters from 27 papers, flags a measurement-validity gap. Snippet-only; unverified. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.05775)
- Replication-style 100-task Verified subset taxonomy (five stages, eleven categories, 657 of 900 succeeded) in search snippet appears to be the same work as 2605.12270 (secondary summary says mini-SWE-agent framework, unconfirmed). Treat as the same paper until verified.

### Inferences
- Existing taxonomies are phase-based (localize / repair / iterate or understand / strategy / logic) and attribute most failures to reasoning; none of the fetched ones carve out decoding pathologies (loops/truncation) or patch-application/format failures as first-class categories (Liu et al. has "Non-Progressive Iteration" and "Context Amnesia" closest; format failures are mentioned only in the original SWE-bench paper and Aider data). A new small-model taxonomy that adds "degenerate repetition", "malformed edit", and "edit applied only after tolerance" would be additive; this is my inference from the categories listed on pages I read, and the full codebooks (25 subcategories) were not fully visible.
- Frontier-model taxonomies report localization as a low failure share (Jiang et al.), while Liu et al. report localization error in 51.3% of Agentless failures, so the localization share depends heavily on pipeline (Agentless) vs agent and on model; a new taxonomy should state scaffold.

### Gaps
- Full text of 2605.12270 and the complete 25-subcategory codebook of 2509.13941 not read.
- No taxonomy found specifically for small (<=20B) or reasoning-model repair failures including loops and edit-format failures.
- Authors of 2509.13941 not captured by the fetch.
