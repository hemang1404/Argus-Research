## 1. Foundational works: what each shows and assumes

### Takeaway
The foundational papers establish three things under idealized conditions: (a) a cheap-to-expensive cascade with a learned acceptance score can match the best single API model at much lower cost (FrugalGPT, AutoMix); (b) a learned binary router can send a large share of traffic to a weak model with small quality loss (Hybrid LLM, RouteLLM); (c) the whole approach is bounded by the quality of the quality estimator (Dekoninck et al.) and by a "Zero router" baseline that mixes models along the cost-quality convex hull (RouterBench). Most assume a fixed model pool, in-distribution test queries, available quality labels, and negligible router cost.

### Cited Findings

**FrugalGPT** (Chen, Zaharia, Zou; 2023; arXiv preprint, v1 only, arXiv page lists no venue) — FULLTEXT (pdftotext of PDF) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)
- Setting/actions: classification and QA tasks over commercial LLM APIs; actions are an ordered list of LLMs plus per-LLM acceptance thresholds (a learned scorer decides whether to stop or call the next LLM). Signal: a learned answer-quality scorer on the generated answer. Authors also outline prompt adaptation and LLM approximation as other strategies. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)
- Result as stated: matches best individual LLM (GPT-4) with up to 98% cost reduction or beats GPT-4 accuracy by 4% at same cost. Table 3 cost savings to match best single LLM: HEADLINES 98.3% (vs GPT-4), OVERRULING 73.3% (vs GPT-4), COQA 59.2% (vs GPT-3); the text summarises the range as 50% to 98%. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05176)
- Stated limitations: needs labeled examples; training examples must come from the same or similar distribution as test examples; learning the cascade itself costs resources (framed as a one-time upfront cost); latency, fairness, privacy and environmental impact not optimized. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05176)

**Hybrid LLM** (Ding, Mallick, Wang, Sim, Mukherjee, Ruhle, Lakshmanan, Awadallah; 2024; arXiv page says accepted to ICLR 2024 main conference) — FULLTEXT (HTML) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14618)
- Setting: two models, small vs large; pre-generation router predicts whether the small model's quality gap to the large model is within a tolerance; the tolerance is tunable at test time. Signal: query text -> DeBERTa-v3-large router trained on BART-score quality gaps (deterministic, probabilistic, and "transformed" labels). Data: MixInstruct; pairs Llama-2 7b/13b, Llama-2 13b/GPT-3.5-turbo, FLAN-t5 800m/Llama-2 13b. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14618)
- Result as stated: up to 40% fewer large-model calls with no quality drop (abstract). Detail: on 7b/13b, thresholds tuned for <1% drop gave test drops of 1.39-1.60% at 96-98% cost advantage; on 13b/GPT-3.5 drops 0.11-0.55% at only 8-16% cost advantage. Cost advantage = % of queries sent to the small model (not dollars/FLOPs). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14618)
- Stated limitations/assumptions: one dataset, fixed pairs, no OOD test; router trained per model pair (transfer worked when quality gaps correlated r>0.7, failed at r=0.06); router cost assumed negligible; BART-score is the quality proxy (routing worked when BART and GPT-4 gaps correlated r=0.46, decayed at r=0.26). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14618)

**AutoMix** (Aggarwal, Madaan, ... Mausam; arXiv v1 Oct 2023, v5 Jan 2025; NeurIPS 2024 per arXiv comments) — FULLTEXT (HTML, last ~13k chars unread) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12963)
- Setting: cascade from small LM to large LM (GPT-4) for context-grounded QA (qasper, coqa, quality, mutual, diplomat). Signal: few-shot self-verification by the small model (fraction of k sampled "Correct" verdicts), then a POMDP router (observation likelihoods by Gaussian KDE; AdaOps solver) decides keep vs escalate. Reward R = P - lambda*C. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12963)
- Result as stated: >50% cost reduction at comparable performance (abstract; intro says "over two times"). Metric IBC (incremental benefit per cost); best Delta-IBC on diplomat averaged ~122%. POMDP variant was the only method with positive gains over always-large in every config vs FrugalGPT and HybridLLM. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12963)
- Cost accounting is explicit: C = C_SLM + C_verify + w*C_LLM, with verification cost set equal to the small model's cost. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12963)
- Failure cases reported: self-verification weak on quality and qasper (verifier confidence does not track correctness); verifier rejects correct paraphrases and accepts wrong answers; with an uninformative middle-model verifier, gains are negligible or negative; chained routing underperforms. Scope: context-grounded reasoning only, not factual/commonsense QA. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12963)

**RouteLLM** (Ong, Almahairi, Wu, Chiang, Wu, Gonzalez, Kadous, Stoica; v1 Jun 2024, v4 Feb 2025; arXiv page lists no venue) — FULLTEXT (HTML) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)
- Setting: binary routing between a strong model (gpt-4-1106-preview) and weak model (Mixtral 8x7B), pre-generation. Signal: Chatbot Arena human preference data (plus ~120k LLM-judge-labeled augmentation costing ~$700); routers: similarity-weighted ranking, matrix factorization, BERT classifier, causal-LLM classifier (Llama 3 8B). Metric: call-performance threshold (CPT) = min share of strong-model calls to reach x% of performance gap recovered. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665)
- Result as stated: >2x cost reduction without quality loss (abstract); Table 6 cost-saving ratios vs GPT-4: MT Bench 3.66x (CPT50%) / 2.49x (CPT80%) at 95% of GPT-4; MMLU 1.41x / 1.14x at 92%; GSM8K 1.49x / 1.27x at 87%. On MMLU gains required ~1,500 golden-labeled in-domain examples (<2% of data). Routers transferred without retraining to Claude 3 Opus/Sonnet and Llama 3.1 70B/8B pairs on MT Bench (~half the strong-model calls of random at CPT80%). Router overhead: $3-39 per million requests (<=0.4% of GPT-4 generation cost). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665)
- Stated limitations: real query distributions may differ from benchmarks; only two-model routing; large unexplained variance between routers trained on same data; no single best router. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665)

**RouterBench** (Hu, Bieker, Li, Jiang, Keigwin, Ranganath, Keutzer, Upadhyay; Mar 2024; arXiv page lists no venue) — FULLTEXT (HTML) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12031)
- Contents: 405,467 samples, 11 LLMs, 8 datasets/64 tasks (HellaSwag, Winogrande, ARC-C, MMLU, MT-Bench, GSM8K, MBPP (code), and an 800-query RAG set). Metric AIQ = area under the non-decreasing convex hull of the cost-quality plane. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- Findings: oracle router far above all models (headroom); KNN and MLP predictive routers match the best single LLMs at lower or similar cost but "do not significantly outperform" the Zero router (convex-hull mixture baseline); win on MMLU/Winogrande, tie on HellaSwag/GSM8K, underperform on ARC-C and MBPP. Cascading routers beat Zero router and singles only if the judge error rate is up to ~0.1; performance can deteriorate rapidly above ~0.2 error. Costs of comparable-quality services vary 2-5x. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- Assumptions/limitations: cascade results assume an idealized judge with no judge cost; router overhead not explicitly included; only performance and $ cost (no latency); only predictive and cascading routers evaluated. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)

**MixLLM** (Wang, Liu, Cheng, Zhao, Chen, Yu, Fu, Chen; Feb 2025; NAACL 2025 main per arXiv comments) — FULLTEXT (HTML) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18482)
- Setting: contextual-bandit routing over a changing pool of LLMs with quality/cost/latency trade-off. Actions: choose LLM (Top-1/2/3 or dynamic) per query. Signal: tag-enhanced query embedding -> per-LLM quality regressors (random forest), predicted output length for cost, latency penalty, LinUCB-style uncertainty term e^T A^-1 e, softmax policy trained by policy gradient on binary user feedback online. Data: RouterBench (36,497 queries, 11 LLMs) + added Llama 3.1 8B/70B. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18482)
- Result as stated: 97.25% of GPT-4 quality at 24.18% of its cost (lambda=1.4), best baseline OptLLM 96.39% at 32.94%; with Llama 3.1 added, 98.55% at 16.79%. Out-of-domain: -5.44% offline only, -3.35% with online training. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18482)
- Stated limitations: needs refined (quality+cost) feedback; new-domain queries hard; choosing one answer from multiple LLMs unresolved; hierarchical routing unexplored; not tested in real deployment. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18482)

**A Unified Approach to Routing and Cascading for LLMs** (Dekoninck, Baader, Vechev; v1 Oct 2024 ... v5 26 Oct 2026; arXiv page lists no venue) — FULLTEXT (HTML, last ~50k chars unread) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10347)
- Setting: pool of LLMs, per-query quality and cost estimators (ex-ante quality estimate before calling, post-hoc estimate after seeing output), maximize expected quality under a cost budget via a lambda-weighted objective. Actions: any ordered subset ("supermodel") -> "cascade routing" generalizes routing (single model) and cascading (sequence). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- Theory: Thm 1 optimal routing = convex combination of cheapest/most expensive models achieving best tradeoff; Thm 2 optimal cascade has same form with per-step lambda_j; Thm 3 cascade routing likewise; Corollary 1: the usual threshold cascade equals the optimal one only under specific estimator conditions. Assumes finitely many estimate values and a feasible budget; cost estimation is "less critical" than quality estimation; selection overhead assumed negligible and router/estimator cost not clearly included in budget. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- Quantitative: on RouterBench cascade routing beats baselines by ~1-4% AUC (13-80% relative to linear interpolation) and beats threshold cascade by up to 2%; noise sweep up to 8% over cascading and 12% over routing; SWE-Bench up to 14% over baselines; on Minerva Math + LiveCodeBench routing beats baseline cascade by 11% and cascade routing only matches routing "because post-hoc estimates are poor"; poor-estimator tasks (classification, open-form) gains <=1.2%. Estimator quality is the key factor: routing depends on ex-ante noise, cascading on post-hoc noise; gains shrink under high noise. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- Version note: the arXiv abstract page (current v5) frames the work around agentic systems; the page I read has no changelog, so I cannot say what changed in v4 (12 Oct 2026) and v5 (26 Oct 2026). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10347)

### Inferences
- FrugalGPT, Hybrid LLM and AutoMix report savings against "always use the biggest model", which RouterBench shows is a weak reference: the right bar is the convex-hull Zero router, and predictive routers often only match it (RouterBench). Headline "X% cheaper" numbers from these papers therefore overstate marginal value over a tuned mixture or single cheaper model.
- Every foundational paper's guarantee passes through a quality estimator (learned scorer, self-verification, BART-score, human-preference proxy, judge). That is the shared single point of failure (see Q4).

### Gaps
- I did not verify later publication venues for FrugalGPT, RouteLLM or Dekoninck et al. (arXiv pages list none); do not cite a venue for them from these notes.

## 2. 2025 to Nov 2026 literature: learned routers, bandits, cascades with estimators, RL routers, cluster-route-escalate, surveys

### Takeaway
The newer work moves (i) to bandit/online learning with budget constraints and regret bounds, (ii) to RL-trained routers/cascade policies, (iii) to cluster-based, training-light routers, (iv) to agentic/step-level routing, and (v) to benchmarks that expose how little routers beat strong baselines. Established: complementarity between models is real and an oracle gap is large. Contested: whether learned routers beat a simple best single model, kNN, or a tuned cascade; evidence is mixed and setup-dependent.

### Cited Findings

#### Surveys and characterizations
- **Dynamic Model Routing and Cascading for Efficient LLM Inference: A Survey** (Moslem, Kelleher; arXiv 2603.04445; v1 23 Feb 2026, v2 21 Apr 2026, v3 30 Aug 2026; accepted by TMLR per arXiv comments; cs.NI primary) — ABSTRACT only. Covers routing paradigms: query difficulty, human preferences, clustering, uncertainty quantification, RL, multimodality, cascading; characterizes systems by when decisions are made, what information is used, how computed. States well-designed routing can outperform the strongest single model; generalization across architectures/modalities/applications is open. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04445)
- **Towards Efficient Multi-LLM Inference: Characterization and Analysis of LLM Routing and Hierarchical Techniques** (Behera, Champati, Morabito, Tarkoma, Gross; arXiv 2506.06579; 6 Jun 2025; v1 only; no venue listed) — ABSTRACT only. It is a survey/characterization of routing (pick one model) and cascading/hierarchical inference (escalate until confident), comparing them on key performance metrics, discussing benchmarking and open challenges, motivated by mobile/edge/cost-sensitive settings. Not an empirical measurement study as far as the abstract shows. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06579)

#### Bandit / online routers
- **Adaptive LLM Routing under Budget Constraints (PILOT)** (Panda, Magazine, Devaguptapu, Takemori, Sharma; arXiv 2508.21141; Aug-Sep 2025; EMNLP 2025 Findings per arXiv) — ABSTRACT only (plus a search-snippet claim, unverified, that the paper lists lack of budget constraints during online learning as a limitation). Contextual bandit: shared query/LLM embedding initialized from offline human preference data, refined online with LinUCB (PILOT); budget handled by an online multi-choice knapsack cost policy. Abstract gives no numbers. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21141)
- **Near-Optimal Online Deployment and Routing for Streaming LLMs (StageRoute)** (Li, Li; arXiv 2506.17254; v1 Jun 2025, v2 Jan 2026; ICLR 2026 per arXiv page) — ABSTRACT only. Hierarchical: select up to M_max models per stage by optimistic bounds (reward UCB, cost LCB), then route each query via a budget- and throughput-constrained bandit subproblem. Regret O~(T^{2/3}) with a matching lower bound; "tracks a strong oracle under tight budgets". — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17254)
- **Online Multi-LLM Selection via Contextual Bandits under Unstructured Context Evolution** (Poon, Dai, Liu, Kong, Lui, Zuo; arXiv 2506.17670; Jun 2025; v1; arXiv page lists no venue; a search snippet attributes AAAI 2026, unverified) — ABSTRACT only. Multi-step refinement where the prompt changes after each model output; LinUCB-based algorithm with "myopic regret" and sublinear-regret claim, with budget-aware and position-aware extensions. No numbers in abstract. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17670)
- **UniRoute / Universal Model Routing for Efficient LLM Inference** (Jitkrittum et al., Google; arXiv 2502.08773; Feb 2025, v2 Jul 2025; no venue listed) — ABSTRACT only. Dynamic pool: each LLM represented by its prediction errors on representative prompts; cluster-based and learned-cluster-map routers; excess-risk bound; effective routing among "more than 30 unseen LLMs". — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08773)
- **Router-R1** (Zhang, Feng, You; arXiv 2506.09033; Jun 2025, v3 Oct 2025; NeurIPS 2025 per arXiv) — ABSTRACT only. RL-trained LLM router that interleaves "think" and "route" actions across multiple rounds with aggregation; rule-based reward = format + outcome + cost; conditions on model descriptors (price, latency, example performance); beats strong baselines on seven general and multi-hop QA benchmarks (no numbers in abstract). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09033)
- **Agent-as-a-Router / ACRouter, CodeRouterBench** (Zhou et al., 11 authors; arXiv 2606.22902; v1 22 Jun 2026, v3 26 Jun 2026; no venue; "living technical report") — FULLTEXT (HTML, last ~29k chars unread). Streaming/regret setting over 8 frontier LLMs. Orchestrator (fine-tuned Qwen3.5-0.8B + heuristics, weighted vote) + Verifier + online kNN Memory. Reward r = s - 0.1*kappa (kappa in USD). Table: ID cumulative regret ACRouter 205.5 vs DimensionBest 277.4, LinUCB 296.9, LinTS 307.4, LogReg 284.4, Always-Opus-4.6 387.1; OOD (agentic, 176 tasks incl. SWE-bench Verified) ACRouter 17.0 regret / 62.5 perf vs Always-Opus 26.7 / 57.14. Static classifiers (LogReg) collapse OOD (19.64 perf, below random in places). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22902)

#### Learned / preference / cluster / kNN routers
- **Avengers-Pro (Beyond GPT-5: Making LLMs Cheaper and Better via Performance-Efficiency Optimized Routing)** (Zhang, Li, Chen, Zhang, Ye, Bai, Hu; arXiv 2508.12631; v1 Aug 2025, v2 Oct 2025; accepted to DAI 2025 per arXiv) — ABSTRACT only. Embed + cluster queries, route to model with best performance-efficiency score per cluster, alpha trades accuracy vs cost. Across 6 benchmarks and 8 models: +7% average accuracy over GPT-5-medium; matches its accuracy at 27% lower cost; ~90% of its performance at 63% lower cost; Pareto frontier. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12631)
- **Rethinking Predictive Modeling for LLM Routing: When Simple kNN Beats Complex Learned Routers** (Li; arXiv 2505.12601; v1 May 2025, v2 14 May 2026; single author; no venue listed) — FULLTEXT (HTML, last ~32k chars unread). kNN over BERT [CLS] (text) / VLM2Vec (multimodal) embeddings averaging neighbours' scores and costs; benchmarks AlpacaEval, Open LLM Leaderboard v2, HELM-Lite, RouterBench, new vision-language set. Average AUC (text): Oracle 64.91, Random 40.35, kNN k=100 52.68, Linear 53.14 (highest learned), MLP-MF 52.88, Graph 51.82. So on text kNN is NOT the top method on average, which makes the title/abstract ("often outperforms") a limited claim. Multimodal: kNN k=100 72.12 (highest avg). OOD (cross-dataset, 36 pairs): kNN k=100 drops 76.54 -> 73.91 (-2.63), Linear-MF -6.67. Authors' stated caveat: complex methods may help on long-tail/sparse-neighbourhood, fast-changing performance, compositional tasks; kNN memory grows linearly. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12601)
- **RouterXBench / ProbeDirichlet** (Wu et al.; arXiv 2602.11877; Feb 2026; v1; no venue) — ABSTRACT only. Evaluation along router ability, scenario alignment, cross-domain robustness; hidden-state-based router ProbeDirichlet reports 16.68% relative gain over best baseline in router ability and 18.86% in high-accuracy scenarios. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11877)
- **RouterArena** (Lu et al.; arXiv 2510.00202; v1 Sep 2025, v3 Nov 2025; arXiv comments do not mention a venue; a search result lists an ICLR 2026 poster page, not confirmed on arXiv) — ABSTRACT only. Open platform: dataset with domains and difficulty levels, metrics, automated leaderboard. No numbers in the abstract. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00202)
- **LLMRouterBench** — see Q4 (benchmark; key negative finding).

#### Cascades with quality estimators
- **Language Model Cascades: Token-level uncertainty and beyond** (Gupta et al., Google; arXiv 2404.10136; Apr 2024; no venue) — ABSTRACT only. Sequence-level uncertainty has length bias; learned post-hoc deferral rules on token-level uncertainty beat simple aggregation on FLAN-T5; adding small-model embeddings and larger-model intermediate-layer features helps further. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10136)
- **Is Escalation Worth It? On the Depth of LLM Cascades** (Bouchard; arXiv 2605.06350; v1 7 May 2026 titled "...A Decision-Theoretic Characterization of LLM Cascades", v2 5 Oct 2026, "substantial revision"; arXiv page states no venue) — FULLTEXT of current v2 HTML (last ~93k chars unread) + abstract page of v1. v2: pool of 8 models, 5 benchmarks (MATH, MMLU, TriviaQA, SimpleQA, LiveCodeBench), 9 deferral scores (5 from log-probs, 4 learned), cost cumulative (a query that escalates pays every model it reaches). Two-model cascades beat the best budget-feasible single model by 2.1-8.2 pp mean test accuracy; allowing >2 models did not improve mean test accuracy in 118 of 135 comparisons (at most +0.43 pp); counterfactual high-AUROC synthetic scores make a third stage help on 4/5 benchmarks but gains vanish when scores also reflect difficulty shared across models. Stated limitation: only deterministic threshold cascades deferring on each model's own output; adaptive routing and router-cascade hybrids were not tested; one pool, binary correctness. — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06350)
  - v1 vs v2 discrepancy: the v1 abstract page (as summarised by the fetch tool) said a lightweight pre-generation router beat the best cascade policy on four of five datasets, attributed mainly to cost structure (the router skips the cheap model's generation cost), and that optimized subsequence cascades gave no meaningful held-out gain over the best pairwise cascade. The current v2 text I read contains no router-vs-cascade experiment, only a mention of routers in related work. So the v1 "router beats cascade" claim should not be cited as a current result. — [v1 abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06350v1); [v2 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06350)
- **Conformal Cascade: Distribution-Free Accuracy Guarantees for Multi-Tier LLM Inference** (Dou, Lian, Li; arXiv 2607.25018; v1 27 Jul 2026, v3 31 Jul 2026; no venue) — ABSTRACT only. Defers when the conformal prediction set is not a singleton; coverage >= 1-K*alpha (tightening to 1-alpha under a selection-preservation condition); 18 multiple-choice benchmarks, 4 open-weight families, two tiers; beats the strongest calibration-tuned heuristic cascade on most family-benchmark pairs. Open-ended generation left for future work. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25018)
- **RouteNLP** (Guo, Wu, Yiu; arXiv 2604.23577; 26 Apr 2026; ACL 2026 Industry Track per arXiv) — ABSTRACT only. Difficulty-aware router + conformal cascading + distillation loop; claims 40-85% cost reduction on six tasks and, in an 8-week pilot (~5K queries/day), 58% cost reduction, 91% acceptance, p99 latency 1,847 -> 387 ms. Enterprise pilot numbers are self-reported. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23577)
- **R2V Agent: Teaching SLMs When to Ask for Help** (Hemadri et al.; arXiv 2605.16604; 15 May 2026; no venue) — ABSTRACT only. Step-level small-LM/teacher routing using Brier-calibrated residual-failure-risk estimate with a CVaR constraint; reported HumanEval+ 94.3% success at 0.60% LLM escalation; TextWorld 64.6% -> 98.2% at 41.7% escalation; TerminalBench 93.3% at 33.9% LLM calls (~half the cost of a heuristic router). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16604)

#### RL routers
- **RLCascadeRouter: Quality-Estimator-Free Cascade Routing via Reinforcement Learning** (Huang, Wang, Ma, Xu; arXiv 2608.15817; 16 Aug 2026; v1 only; no venue; cs.AI) — FULLTEXT (HTML). MDP: actions = Stop or any not-yet-selected model (max 3 calls); state = query, current response, selected-model history, accumulated cost, depth; reward = change in U_alpha = alpha*Q + (1-alpha)*C; PPO, gamma=1; Cascade Policy Network = Complementarity Encoder (self-attention over valid actions) + Value-Aware Stopper (stop gate + critic). Data: ten LLMRouterBench benchmarks, 13 LLMs, 60/10/30 split seed 42. Baselines: 13 single models + Random, HybridLLM, FrugalGPT, GraphRouter, Avengers-Pro. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15817)
  - Numbers (alpha=1.0): RLCascadeRouter macro-avg 68.81% at cost 180.22; Avengers-Pro 67.84% at 135.65 (+0.97 pt); best single GPT-5 65.12% at 124.81 (+3.69 pt); FrugalGPT 66.36% at 124.31. RLCascadeRouter has the HIGHEST cost among routers in Table 1. Cost-performance frontier claims: 59.11% at 10.64 vs Avengers-Pro 59.10% at 16.12 (34% cheaper); 67.29% at 85.41 vs 67.22% at 107.88 (20.8% cheaper); a point beating GPT-5 by 2.17 pt at 31.6% lower cost. Coding: LiveCodeBench 84.23 (best 84.54); SWE-bench 36.00 (ties best routing result). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15817)
  - Does it beat a tuned cascade? Not tested directly: no tuned threshold cascade baseline; the only estimator-based comparison is its own "QE Stop" ablation (calibrated quality-estimator threshold inside the same framework: 67.70 vs 68.81). Dekoninck et al. is discussed conceptually but not run as a baseline; Proposition 1 is a constructed counterexample, not an empirical test. No dedicated limitations section. Margins over Avengers-Pro (<1 pt at alpha=1) come from single split, single seed as described. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15817)
- **HierRouter** (PPO routing across specialized models, arXiv 2511.09873) — SNIPPET only (search result); not fetched; do not cite details.

#### Cluster-route-escalate
- **Cluster, Route, Escalate (CRE-Router): Cascaded Framework for Cost-Aware LLM Serving** (Moslem + 13 authors incl. Kelleher; arXiv 2606.27457; v1 25 Jun 2026, v2 23 Oct 2026; no venue; cs.PF; code github.com/ymoslem/CRE-Router) — FULLTEXT (PDF via pdftotext; v2). Stage 1: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings, k-means; each cluster assigned to the model with best error-cost balance subject to a TPOT (time per output token) or end-to-end-latency budget, using only task-correctness labels, with Pareto pruning. Stage 2: ModernBERT-base binary quality estimator accepts/rejects the efficient model's answer, escalating to the strong model. Benchmarks TeleMath, TeleQnA, AIME; local open-weight pools of 2-9 models on A100s. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27457)
  - Results: on TeleMath (201 test questions), at TPOT budget 20 ms CRE-Router (Stage 1+2) 65.0% / 18.8 ms vs HybridLLM 56.8%, FrugalGPT 50.9%; at 25 ms 67.4% / ~21 ms vs strong model Qwen3-30B-think alone 67.3% / 29.0 ms (about 26% lower TPOT at equal accuracy); at least ten points more accurate than every pool model with lower TPOT. Less accurate than Gemma4-26B-think (72.4%) but 47-52% lower TPOT. Estimator ablation: vs escalating the same number of answers at random, gain is +1.7 pp [-0.1, +3.5] on TeleMath (not significant), +3.0 [+1.6, +4.4] TeleQnA, +6.1 [+2.6, +10.2] AIME; on AIME escalating the longest answers does as well as the estimator. The choice of cost term (TPOT vs E2EL) changes which model is selected; hardware changes shift the best routing. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27457)
  - Stated limitations: needs task-correctness labels (verifiable tasks); clusters/routings are fitted offline and do not adapt to query-distribution shift; budget bounds train-set Stage 1 cost so test cost can exceed it; A100-only; the estimator judges whether the efficient answer is correct, not whether the strong model can fix it, so some escalations are wasted. Router overhead is stated negligible and excluded from reported costs. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27457)

### Inferences
- "Learned router beats a well-tuned cascade or simple threshold" is contested, not established. Evidence for: Dekoninck (cascade routing > routing and > threshold cascade by up to 2% AUC on RouterBench, with ideal-ish estimators), RLCascadeRouter (+1 pt over Avengers-Pro, +2.4 over FrugalGPT, own benchmarks, at higher cost), CRE-Router (> reimplemented HybridLLM/FrugalGPT on three tasks, single hardware). Evidence against / qualifying: RouterBench (KNN/MLP routers don't significantly beat Zero router), LLMRouterBench (see Q4), kNN paper (average AUC essentially tied across linear/MLP/kNN), Bouchard v2 (depth beyond 2 rarely helps; threshold cascades with log-prob scores are a strong baseline), CRE ablation (estimator gain over random escalation not significant on TeleMath), Resample-or-Reroute v3 (outcome-blind selectors don't beat fixed action). No fetched paper runs a fair three-way comparison {tuned threshold cascade, learned router, cascade routing} with matched cost accounting on the same data.
- The newer RL router claims (RLCascadeRouter) are on LLMRouterBench data, where the benchmark's own authors report most routers fail to beat Best Single; RLCascadeRouter's +3.69 pt over GPT-5 is paid for with +44% cost at alpha=1.
- Established (multiple independent sources): large oracle headroom and model complementarity (LLMRouterBench, RouterBench); estimator quality governs outcomes (Dekoninck, RouterBench judge-error result, AutoMix failure cases).

### Gaps
- Preprints from 2026 are mostly single-version arXiv postings with no venue; I confirmed peer review only where the arXiv page says so (TMLR for the survey; ICML-workshop for SWE-Router; ICLR 2026 for StageRoute; NeurIPS 2025 for Router-R1; EMNLP 2025 Findings for PILOT; NAACL 2025 for MixLLM; ACL 2026 Industry for RouteNLP; Findings-ACL-2026 for LLMRouterBench is from a search snippet only, not arXiv).
- PILOT, StageRoute, Poon et al., UniRoute, Router-R1, RouterXBench, RouterArena, R2V, Conformal Cascade, RouteNLP, both surveys: abstract only; method detail/limitations unverified.
- 2603.04445 survey's own claims about which paradigms win were not extracted beyond the abstract.

## 3. Verifiers / execution feedback / code benchmarks, and Resample or Reroute (arXiv 2607.08665)

### Takeaway
Few routing papers use real executable verification; most use a learned or self-generated quality proxy. The code/SWE-bench-relevant ones are: EcoAssistant and Model Cascading for Code (execution or self-tests gate escalation), SWE-Router (unit-test-derived labels, partial-trajectory value head), TwinRouterBench (execution-verified step labels, live SWE-bench Verified), Triage (SWE-bench Lite), ACRouter/CodeRouterBench (sandbox-execution verifier, SWE-bench Verified OOD), and Resample-or-Reroute (EvalPlus/test-based weak verifier on MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, BigCodeBench). Resample-or-Reroute v3 is an explicit negative result: after a weak verifier accepts, there is measurable recoverable accuracy, but no tested outcome-blind selector beat the fixed reroute action.

### Cited Findings

#### Resample or Reroute (arXiv 2607.08665) — FULLTEXT of v1 HTML and v3 HTML (v3 final ~4k chars unread; v2 not fetched separately; v1 PDF title read from PDF metadata only)
- Identity: single author Teng-Ruei Chen, cs.LG. v1 9 Jul 2026, v2 10 Jul 2026, v3 2 Sep 2026 (current). No withdrawal note on the page; listed as active. arXiv comments for v3: 33 pages (IEEEtran two-column) incl. 14-page supplement, 14 figures, 15 tables; v3 is described as a "substantive methodological reconstruction" of v1-v2; v2 corrected the Phi-4 cost to 14.7B. Title: v3 = "Resample or Reroute? Recoverable Stopping Debt Without Identified Action Selection"; v1 PDF metadata title = "Resample or Reroute? Budget-Aware Test-Time Model Selection for Large Language Models" (the v1 HTML extract showed the shorter "Resample or Reroute?" heading). Not peer reviewed. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08665); [v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08665v1); [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- **v1 (as read from v1 HTML)**: resampling a committed model vs rerouting to another model treated as competing uses of one per-query cost budget B under an imperfect verifier. Action: resample an already-used model or reroute (first draw from a new model); stop when budget spent, return verifier-selected answer. Policy "RoR": Bayesian posterior mean of per-model per-query success (prior pseudo-count s=2) with greedy marginal-correctness-per-cost rule; UCB variant; non-deployable oracle. Data: offline replay on GSM8K (500), MATH-500 (500), GPQA-Diamond (198), HumanEval+ (164); pool of 11 open-weight models (Mistral, Qwen 2.5 + R1 distill, Phi-4, OLMo-2, Yi-1.5, Granite-3.3, Gemma-2, Llama-3.1); k=30 draws per (query, model) at T=0.2; 50/50 split, 20 draw orderings. Cost proxy = parameter count (B), with a provider-price robustness replay. Baselines: single-route, one-commit learned router, budget-aware best-of-K, FrugalGPT-style cascade, random allocation. Claims: RoR dominates or matches budget-scalable baselines on GSM8K/MATH-500/GPQA, e.g. GPQA 0.968 at cost 25.9 vs best-of-K 0.861, cascade 0.926, one-commit router 0.566, single-route 0.551; HumanEval+ 0.952 at 20.5 (random 0.959 at 25.8; cascade 0.952; best-of-K 0.852). - **v3**: the budget-allocation, learned-router, retrospective cost-oracle and broad Pareto claims were removed and replaced by a three-gate identification framework; the 11-model replay was reclassified as audit/provenance evidence (HumanEval+, GSM8K, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond "historical audit, provenance only"); new fresh experiments on MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, BigCodeBench were added. (The v2-to-v3 delta is stated in the arXiv comments and in v3's own text; I did not fetch v2 separately, and v1-to-v2 is only known to be the Phi-4 cost correction.) — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08665); [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- **v3 setting/actions/signal**: after a weak verifier (EvalPlus Base tests) accepts a response, a second call must be either resample (another draw from Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-AWQ) or reroute (one draw from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ). Hidden correctness = Base+Plus tests (evaluator-only). Decision signal = observable history only ("outcome-blind selector", controllers L1-L4 using runtime/hash features). Three gates, failure at any gate absorbing: (1) recoverable stopping debt; (2) two-sided FIT action support (both reroute-favoring and resample-favoring episodes in fitting bank at preregistered minimums); (3) held-out value of an outcome-blind selector over the frozen best fixed action. Gates are not composed across datasets. — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- **v3 numbers**: MBPP+ v0.2.0 via EvalPlus 0.3.1, 378 queries, hash split -> 152 TEST queries x 10 draws (3,040 scored candidates). Gate 1: 7B recovery from 14B Base-only false-positive stops +2.592 pp (95% CI [+1.618, +3.664], 10,000-replicate cluster bootstrap); same-model control (14B resample) +2.039 pp; secondary fixed reroute to 14B minus leave-one-out 7B resample +2.882 pp [+0.931, +5.201] (underpowered). Full-correct rates 7B 68.36%, 14B 68.55%; Base-only false-positive stops 12.30% / 13.03%. Only 28 of 152 query clusters contribute nonzero recovery; top ten hold 60.2%. Robustness (post-outcome) Llama-3.1-8B +2.974 pp and Nemotron-Nano-9B +3.303 pp. All-episode audit: realized-maximum gap +2.697 pp [+1.711, +3.816] over cross-fitted fixed reroute, but it falls at the 18.283rd percentile of an exchangeable-swap reference (mean +3.158 pp), and the statistic equals 1/2 E|Delta| - 1/2|E Delta| for two actions so it contains no observable-history signal. Controllers vs fixed reroute: 0.000 pp [-0.987, +1.053] and -0.197 pp [-1.184, +0.789]. — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- LiveCodeBench v5+v6 (342-task manifest; 136 FIT/68 DEV/137 TEST; 70 eligible TEST queries): FIT has 66 incorrect stops and zero rescues, so all action-advantage labels are zero; L1-L4 make zero deviations and tie the resample baseline (0.595238; fixed reroute 0.611905; evaluator-only max 0.621429). BigCodeBench v0.1.4 (1,100 tasks; 483 FIT/211 DEV/405 TEST): preregistered gate fails by one query/episode-ish margin (reroute-favoring 23 episodes/19 queries vs minimum 25/20; resample-favoring 22/19), verdict STOP_INSUFFICIENT_TWO_SIDED_FIT_SUPPORT, L1-L4/DEV/TEST never opened. Descriptive post-gate only: reroute uses ~170 fewer generated tokens per draw (339.7 vs 509.7). SWE-bench is NOT used. — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- **What the negative result means (author's framing)**: "stopping debt exists, but current evidence does not identify when to resample rather than reroute"; Gate 1 "licenses recovery, not policy"; failure does not prove the actions are equivalent or that features are uninformative (BigCodeBench shortfall is relative to a preregistered standard, "not a natural constant"); evidence-supported output "remains a fixed action or a stopped experiment"; adding a bigger model or static routing after a failed gate is "a new study, not a repair"; practitioners should not read it as permission to deploy a dynamic router. — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- **Stated limitations (v3)**: MBPP+ TEST opening was self-authorized with no external review; evaluator source changed after the first sealed computation (decimal-serialization fix; scoring byte-identical); one benchmark family for primary result and a single 14B/7B pair; robustness arms specified after primary outcome and share queries and the Qwen14 stop bank; cost symbolic only (no production cost vector; excludes prefill, verifier, queueing, dollar, energy); finite banks cannot establish population identification. — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)

- **EcoAssistant** (Zhang, Krishna, Awadallah, Wang; arXiv 2310.03046; Oct 2023; v1; no venue) — ABSTRACT only. Code-driven API queries; iterative refinement via automatic code executor + hierarchy of cheaper-to-stronger LLMs + retrieval of past successes as demonstrations; reports +10 points success over GPT-4 at <50% of GPT-4's cost. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03046)

- **SWE-Router** (Son, Yoon, Tang, Wang, Wolf, Bogunovic; arXiv 2607.00053; 30 Jun 2026; Deep Learning for Code Workshop at ICML 2026) — FULLTEXT (HTML). ### Inferences
- The only papers in this set whose verifier is a ground-truth executable oracle during routing are SWE-Router (labels at train time, not available at inference), TwinRouterBench (labels), and Resample-or-Reroute (Base tests as the runtime verifier, Plus tests hidden). At deploy time, most code-routing systems rely on partial signals (self-generated tests, sandbox execution without oracle tests, public tests). Resample-or-Reroute is the only one that studies explicitly the post-verifier-acceptance residual error (false-positive stops) as a quantity; its 12-13% Base-only false-positive stop rate and ~2.6 pp recoverable gain are specific to EvalPlus Base-vs-Plus on MBPP+ with one Qwen pair.
- v1 -> v3 of Resample-or-Reroute is a retraction-in-spirit of the policy-superiority claims (Pareto dominance over cascades and learned routers on HumanEval+ etc.): anyone citing v1/v2 numbers (e.g. "RoR 0.968 on GPQA") should not, since the author reclassified them as provenance. It is a single-author preprint with self-authorized test-bank opening; its negative result is about identification under a preregistered support threshold and a single model pair, not evidence that resample-vs-reroute selection is impossible.
- For SWE-bench-scale routing, step-level or trajectory-conditioned routers (SWE-Router, TwinRouterBench, ACRouter) are the live direction; per-query pre-generation routers are argued to be a poor fit for multi-step tasks (TwinRouterBench's framing; SWE-Router abstract says task-description-only routers cannot distinguish a typo from a multi-module refactor).

- No SWE-bench result with a runtime ground-truth verifier driving escalation was found in the fetched set; TwinRouterBench dynamic result is 100 locked instances (Django/Astropy only).
- Triage, EcoAssistant, Model Cascading for Code, R2V Agent, RAV: abstract only.

## 4. Known failure modes of routers

### Takeaway
Four recurring, evidenced failure modes: (1) routers frequently fail to beat the best single model or a trivial baseline under unified evaluation; (2) performance degrades under distribution shift, with non-parametric/simple routers degrading least; (3) quality-estimator error is the dominant bottleneck and can invert cascade benefits; (4) cost accounting is often incomplete (router/judge/verifier cost, failed-attempt cost, caching, price snapshots, wrong cost term).

### Cited Findings

**Router cannot beat best single model / baselines**
- LLMRouterBench (Li et al.; arXiv 2601.07206; 12 Jan 2026; v1; no venue on arXiv page — a search-result listing indicates Findings of ACL 2026, unconfirmed on arXiv) — FULLTEXT (HTML, last ~32k chars unread): >400K instances, 21 datasets, 33 models, 10 baselines (RouterDC, EmbedLLM, Model-SAT, HybridLLM, RouteLLM, FrugalGPT, GraphRouter, Avengers, Avengers-Pro, OpenRouter). Many routers perform similarly; several recent approaches including commercial routers do not reliably beat a simple Best Single baseline; large oracle gap attributed to persistent model-recall failures (queries where only one model is correct and the router misses it; e.g. on 410 queries (11.9% of test set) with at most three correct models, Avengers 24.6% and EmbedLLM 23.2% accuracy). Performance-cost: top methods up to ~4% higher accuracy than Best Single (GPT-5) or up to 31.7% cost reduction at matched performance; binary routers (HybridLLM, FrugalGPT) struggle to save cost without losing accuracy; OpenRouter -24.7% relative to Best Single. Authors' limitations: subset of methods, 21 common datasets only, approximate single-provider latency. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07206)
- RouterBench: predictive routers do not significantly beat the Zero router. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- Resample-or-Reroute v3: outcome-blind controllers tie fixed reroute (0.000 pp). — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- ACRouter: reported standalone GPT-5.4 above ACRouter on the OOD agentic split. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22902)
- kNN paper: average AUC of learned routers within ~1.5 points of kNN on text; oracle 64.9 vs best learned ~53. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12601)

**Distribution shift**
- FrugalGPT: needs training data from the same distribution as test. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05176)
- kNN paper: OOD drop 2.63 pts (kNN k=100) vs 6.67 (Linear-MF). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12601)
- MixLLM: -5.44% OOD offline, -3.35% with online updates. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18482)
- ACRouter: static classifiers (LogReg) collapse OOD, sometimes below random. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22902)
- SWE-Router: gpt-5-mini router doesn't beat baselines on SWE-Smith test, attributed to shift. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- Lightweight routers on BFCL degrade under held-out task-family shift; transfer from single-step to multi-step also limited. — [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170676)
- CRE-Router: clusters/routings fitted offline, do not adapt to query-distribution shift (author-stated). — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27457)
- Hybrid LLM: transfer to other model pairs depends on correlation of quality gaps. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14618)

**Quality-estimator error**
- Dekoninck: gains shrink with noise; estimator quality is the key factor. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- RouterBench: cascade benefit holds for judge error up to ~0.1 and deteriorates rapidly above ~0.2. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- AutoMix: weak self-verification on some datasets; uninformative verifier nullifies gains. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12963)
- Hybrid LLM: reliance on BART-score as proxy; gains decay when it decorrelates from GPT-4 gap. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14618)
- Bouchard v2: confidence scores that also encode query difficulty shared across models remove the benefit of deeper cascades even at high AUROC. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06350)
- CRE-Router: estimator not significantly better than random escalation on TeleMath; on AIME, escalating longest answers does as well. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27457)
- Lightweight BFCL routers: 88% precision / 13% recall for deferral. — [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170676)
- Resample-or-Reroute v3: weak verifier (Base tests) leaves 12-13% false-positive stops. — [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)
- RLCascadeRouter argues prediction error need not map to decision error (hence "quality-estimator-free") but did not test this against Dekoninck et al. empirically. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15817)
- SWE-Router: AUROC "not strongly correlated with routing performance" (i.e., standard classifier metric can mislead). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)

**Cost accounting omissions or traps**
- Hybrid LLM: router cost assumed negligible; "cost advantage" is % queries to small model, not dollars/FLOPs/energy. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14618)
- RouterBench: cascade judge assumed free; router overhead not explicit. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- Dekoninck: budget defined over model costs only; selection overhead assumed negligible. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- CRE-Router: reported costs exclude router overhead (stated negligible); per-token (TPOT) budget is blind to answer length and picks reasoning models that make requests slow; best routing changes with hardware and cost term. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27457)
- LLMRouterBench: total inference cost per configuration at API prices ($2,771.84 API cost for the study); whether router training/inference overhead is in the cost metric is not clear from the text; notes RouteLLM overhead negligible. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07206)
- RouterArena: lists router overhead as a latency metric, separate from answer cost (inferred from search-result description, SNIPPET-level, unverified). — [search result summary of](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00202)
- AutoMix (counter-example, explicit): charges verification cost. Bouchard v2: cascade cost cumulative incl. cheap-model generations, but excludes self-consistency/verbalized/LLM-judge scores because they need extra calls. — [AutoMix](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12963); [Bouchard](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06350)
- TwinRouterBench: realized spend with cache-write accounting and flat $0.60 per unresolved instance; notes naive per-step cost accounting overstates conservative routers' savings because the high tier is 10-50x pricier; labels depend on price snapshot. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18859)
- ACRouter: provider-side cache hit rates unobservable so cost figures treated as secondary. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22902)
- RLCascadeRouter: highest cost among routers in main table (180.22 vs 124.81 for GPT-5), so "better" depends on alpha. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15817)
- Resample-or-Reroute v1 used parameter count as the cost proxy (with a price-snapshot robustness replay); v3 excludes prefill, verifier, queueing, dollar and energy cost and makes no Pareto claim. — [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v1); [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665v3)

### Inferences
- The cleanest cross-paper pattern: reported savings are conditional on (a) an in-distribution test set, (b) a free or near-free quality signal, (c) cost counted as model API/parameter cost only. Relax any of the three and the evidence (LLMRouterBench, RouterBench, BFCL workshop paper, SWE-Router limitations) shows gains shrink or vanish.
- For an inference-routing system that retries/rescues, the Resample-or-Reroute and CRE-Router results suggest a conservative design: fixed escalation target after a verifier rejection is hard to beat with outcome-blind selection; the estimator should be validated against random escalation and against a trivial feature (e.g. answer length) before adding learned routing machinery.
- The Bouchard v1 "pre-generation router beats cascade because cascades pay the cheap model first" mechanism is a cost-structure effect, not a signal-quality effect; it is consistent with Dekoninck's cascade-routing framework and with TwinRouterBench's step-level routing, but the claim is not in the current v2 text and was not independently reproduced.
