## Q1. Plausible-but-wrong patches: how often does "passes FAIL_TO_PASS" still mean "incorrect", and what does it imply for tests as the verifier in an allocation policy?

### Takeaway
Every independent audit finds that a material minority of test-passing SWE-bench patches are wrong. Estimates range from about 7-8% (patches that fail the full developer test suite), through about 11% (extrapolated PatchDiff), 15.7% (UTBoost on Verified), 4-9 points of resolve rate (augmented-test re-evaluation of top-10 Verified agents), to roughly 30-55% "suspicious" in the older, smaller manual audits. The false-positive rate depends on the audit method, the agent, and the benchmark, so "test pass" is a noisy, positively-biased label, not ground truth.

### Cited Findings

**SWE-bench+ (Aleithan et al.), arXiv 2410.06992, Oct 2024, preprint (no venue listed; v1 9 Oct 2024, v2 10 Oct 2024; not withdrawn). Read: ABS plus FULL-HTML v2.**
- Manual audit of 251 patches from SWE-Agent + GPT-4 that passed all tests (drawn from 286 initially "resolved" instances): 32.67% (82/251) had "solution leakage" (fix outlined in issue or comments) and 31.08% (78/251) were "suspicious" due to weak tests (32 incorrect, 37 incomplete, 9 different file/function). Together 160/251 (63.75%) suspicious. Three authors labeled independently, disagreements settled by discussion, no agreement statistic reported. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2)
- Filtering leaky issues dropped SWE-Agent + GPT-4 from 12.47% to 3.97% (abstract). Body also gives 5.49% for "correct fixes only"; the paper is internally inconsistent here. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992); [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2)
- Applied to Lite: 18 leak instances (33.33% of 54 passed), resolve rate 18% to 9.33%. Applied to Verified: 37 leak instances (33.04% of 112 passed), resolve rate 22.4% to 10.0%. — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2)
- "Over 94%" of issues were created before the LLM knowledge cutoffs (GPT-3.5 Sep 2021, GPT-4 Apr 2023, GPT-4o Oct 2023); the paper does not explain how 94% was computed. — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2)
- SWE-bench+ itself: 548 post-cutoff instances from the same 12 repos minus Django; yet manual review still found about 67.72% of "resolved" instances did not truly resolve the issue; resolution rates 0.55%-3.83% across four systems. Date window stated two ways (Oct 2023 vs 2023-11-01 to 2024-08-22). — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2)
- Limitations: one model's patches only, authors' own labels, small, inconsistent numbers. A search snippet mentioned a "later version" with 33.47%/24.70%, SoluLeakDetector/TestEnhancer and 86% accuracy; arXiv shows only v1/v2, so I could not verify that and do not rely on it.

**"Are 'Solved Issues' in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly?" (Wang, Pradel, Liu), arXiv 2503.15223; ICSE 2026 research track (venue per [ICSE listing](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2026/icse-2026-research-track/59/Are-Solved-Issues-in-SWE-bench-Really-Solved-Correctly-An-Empirical-Study)); v1 19 Mar 2025, v2 9 Sep 2025. Read: FULL-HTML v2 plus ABS.**
- Subjects: plausible patches from CodeStory, LearnByInteract, OpenHands on SWE-bench Verified (877 plausible patches across tools). PatchDiff = differential patch testing; GPT-4o-mini generates differentiating tests with up to 2 repair rounds, flaky tests filtered by 20 reruns. — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15223v2)
- Abstract: 7.8% of all patches count as "correct" in SWE-bench while failing the developer-written (full) test suite; 29.6% of plausible patches behave differently from the oracle patch; of those divergences 46.8% are similar-but-divergent implementations and 27.3% change more behavior than the oracle; 28.6% of divergent patches are "certainly incorrect" (22 of 77 manually sampled); resolution rates inflated by 6.2 absolute points (abstract page). The HTML-extraction pass reported 6.4; the abs page says 6.2, so quote 6.2. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223); [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15223v2)
- Per tool (resolved as reported / after checking against all developer tests / without suspicious patches): CodeStory 62.2% / 57.0% / 44.0%; LearnByInteract 60.2% / 55.6% / 40.8%; OpenHands 53.0% / 49.2% / 38.6%. Suspicious patches per tool 27.2%-32.2%. Extrapolated about 11.0% of plausible patches are incorrect. — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15223v2)
- Root cause: SWE-bench runs only test files modified in the PR, not the repo's wider developer tests. Authors recommend using all developer tests and note many issue statements are under-specified. — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15223v2)
- Limitations (stated): GPT-4o-mini for test generation, first-author labeling (second author on ambiguous cases), 30% sample of suspicious patches with a 9.39% CI at 95%, only 3 tools, Verified only.

**UTBoost (Yu, Zhu, He, Kang), arXiv 2506.09289, ACL 2025 (per [search result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289); arXiv page itself listed v1 only). Read: FULL-HTML v1.**
- Found 36 task instances with insufficient tests (abstract); body breakdown 23 in Lite (7.7%) and 26 in Verified (5.2%) (sums to 49, so presumably overlap; paper's counting not reconciled by me). — [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09289v1)
- Augmented tests flag 170/599 (28.4%) erroneous patches in Lite and 92/584 (15.7%) in Verified; counting the improved log parser too, 176 (Lite) and 169 (Verified) previously-passed patches were wrong. — [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09289v1)
- Leaderboard rank changes: 18/44 Lite entries (40.9%) and 11/45 Verified entries (24.4%). — [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09289v1)
- Harness defect: the original log parser changed annotations for 164/300 Lite and 271/500 Verified instances (about 54% each); e.g. django__django-15278 had 55 PASS_TO_PASS tests unparsed. — [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09289v1)
- Limitations: tests generated only for instances at least one agent resolved (needs a passing patch to cross-validate), GPT-4o only, two-author manual review.

**"Probe to Generate: Program Variant-Guided Test Augmentation for Repository-Level Repair Benchmarks" (Li, Xu, Wang, Tan, Chen), arXiv 2604.01518, accepted ASE 2026; v1 2 Apr 2026, v2 6 Aug 2026. Read: ABS plus FULL-HTML v2 (all but last ~2.8k chars).**
- 77% of Verified instances admit at least one surviving variant (a mutated/LLM-varied patch that passes existing tests but differs from the oracle); 1,014 validated tests across 211 instances; patch-region line/branch coverage +10.8 / +9.5 points. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01518)
- Re-evaluating the top-10 Verified leaderboard agents with public patches: resolved-rate drops 4.2 to 9.0 points (e.g. live-SWE-agent Claude 4.5 Opus -4.2, TRAE -9.0, Atlassian Rovo Dev -8.0). — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01518v2)
- Gold-patch control: a generated test is kept only if it passes on the reference patch and on behavior-preserving transforms of it (23% of candidate tests removed by the transform check). — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01518v2)
- Limitations: Verified Python only (12 repos), GPT-5-mini only, oracle patch treated as the sole correct implementation (one valid alternative found rejected), possible overlap with LLM training data. The page did not address leakage of test-patch names into issues. (A search snippet called this "STING"; I did not confirm that name.)

**Other audits found:**
- SWE-bench Pro Verified (Zheng et al.), arXiv 2609.08149, v1 8 Sep 2026, v3 9 Oct 2026, preprint. Read: ABS. - Original SWE-bench Verified (OpenAI, Aug 2024): 93 developers annotated 1,699 samples on two 0-3 criteria (issue underspecified; FAIL_TO_PASS tests reject valid solutions); 500 retained. The "68.3% filtered" figure appears only on a third-party wiki (LongTermWiki); the 38.3%/61.1% figures I had in mind were not found in any retrieved source. Read: SNIPPET plus SEC ([OpenAI post](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/) is 403 to the fetch tool; I could not read it).

### Inferences
- Treating pass/fail on F2P+P2P as the label has a false-positive rate of order 5-15% on Verified for strong agents, and this rate is not constant. It is higher for agents that add extra behavior (36.4% of incorrect patches in PatchDiff came from faulty additions), and it varies by repo (Django and sympy over-represented in UTBoost). For an allocation policy that learns "which model/budget succeeds" from test outcomes, the reward is a noisy and potentially biased proxy, and any policy ranking that depends on 1-5 point differences is within the audit-induced uncertainty (4-9 point re-evaluation drops, up to 24% rank churn on the leaderboard).
- Cost/accuracy trade-offs in routing are most distorted where cheaper models more often produce narrow "make the test pass" patches; this is a hypothesis consistent with PatchDiff's overfit categories, not a measured result in these papers.
- A defensible design is to report both the official test label and a stricter label (full developer test suite and/or augmented tests), and to show the allocation conclusion is stable across both.

### Gaps
- No paper I found measures false-positive rate as a function of model size or cost tier (the quantity an allocation policy needs). All audits use frontier-agent patches.
- Primary OpenAI posts (Verified 2024, "why we no longer evaluate" Feb 2026, "separating signal from noise" Jul 2026) were all 403 to the fetch tool; their numbers are secondary only.
- Datacurve's separate May 2026 audit numbers rely on an LLM analyzer; no human validation figure was reported on the page I read.

## Q2. Test leakage and contamination critiques of SWE-bench Verified; what follows for a study using public Verified tasks

### Takeaway
There is converging, multi-method evidence that frontier LLMs have seen Verified (file-path recall with no repo context, patch-text overlap, lower scores on fresher data), plus evidence that issue text sometimes contains the fix, and that agents can fetch the answer via git history or the web. OpenAI stopped reporting Verified in Feb 2026 on contamination and flawed-test grounds. A study with public Verified tasks must assume contamination and treat absolute resolve rates as inflated, relying on within-model relative comparisons and a fresh held-out set for claims.

### Cited Findings

**"The SWE-Bench Illusion" (Liang, Garg, Zilouchian Moghaddam; Purdue/Microsoft), arXiv 2506.12286, versions v1 to v4; NeurIPS 2025 listing exists ([neurips.cc](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/137177)) and an ICSE 2026 SEIP listing; the arXiv v3 page itself named no venue. Read: FULL-HTML v3.**
- Models given only the issue text (no repository) pick the buggy file path with 60-76% accuracy on Verified, 57-71% on full SWE-bench, 50-68% on recent issues from SWE-bench repos ("SWE-Repo"), and below 53% on outside-repo tasks (up to a 47-point gap). Models: GPT-4o (two snapshots), GPT-4.1, o3, o3-mini, o4-mini, Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, Claude 4 Sonnet/Opus. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12286v3)
- Function reproduction (5-gram overlap with gold function): Verified up to 34.9%, full SWE-bench 28.7%, SWE-Bench Extra 18.2%, RefactorBench 18.1%, outside-repo 13.9%. — [HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12286v3)
- Prefix-completion verbatim match on Verified: 11.7% (o3-mini) to 31.6% (Claude 4 Opus); Claude 3.5 Sonnet 12.1%, Claude 3.7 12.3%, Claude 4 Sonnet 21.4%, GPT-4.1 17.4%. — [HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12286v3)
- Limitations (stated or implied): issue length differs across sets, small samples (39 RefactorBench tasks, 75 internal C# tasks), heuristic file filter, n-gram overlap noisy, correlational not proof of contamination. Numbers differ across arXiv versions (v1 reported a smaller gap per a search summary); cite the version.

**"Does SWE-Bench-Verified Test Agent Ability or Model Memory?", arXiv 2512.10218 v1, 11 Dec 2025, workshop target (International Workshop on Agentic Engineering, Apr 2026; DOI/ISBN placeholders, publication status unconfirmed). Read: FULL-HTML v1.**
- Issue-only localization (all ground-truth files found): Claude 3.5 Sonnet 65% / Claude 3.7 63.2% on Verified vs 12.2% / 12% on BeetleBox and 12% / 8% on SWE-rebench (Sep 2025 slice; 17.43% / 19.27% on the Jan 2025 slice). At least one GT file: 72.8% / 71% on Verified vs 43% / 44.6% (BeetleBox). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10218v1)
- Limitations: only two Claude models, BeetleBox vagueness filtering by one author, small SWE-rebench slices (109 and 50 issues), correlational. Claude 4 deliberately excluded because of its later cutoff. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10218v1)

**"Schrödinger's Code Repository: Have LLMs Learned SWE-bench or Memorized It?" (Chen et al.), arXiv 2609.27891, listed as submitted 21 Aug 2026 (the "2609" prefix conflicts with an August date; the page did not explain this), preprint, v1 only. Read: ABS only (fetch showed no numeric results in the abstract).** Qualitative claim: removing familiar repository cues consistently degrades agent performance and raises interaction cost. A search-result snippet quoted ">65% of instances show clear leakage evidence and >18% recallable at patch/test level"; I could NOT confirm this on the abstract page, so treat as unverified. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27891)

**OpenAI "Why we no longer evaluate SWE-bench Verified" (23 Feb 2026 per search result). Read: BLOCKED (403); SEC only.**
- Secondary summary (Pebblous blog): 138 hard tasks that o3 failed across 64 runs were audited; 59.4% had flaws in test design or problem statement; a "35.5% overly narrow tests" figure (denominator ambiguous); GPT-5.2 solved 31 tasks classed "nearly impossible", with one Django task whose test required a parameter the statement never mentions and whose chain of thought contained release-note info. — [Pebblous](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/swe-bench-verified-retired/en/)
- A different secondary summary (aiweekly, via search) says the audit named GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Flash as able to reproduce some gold patches, and "35.5% narrow / 18.8% wide". These two secondary sources conflict on which models and on how contamination was shown, so do not state specifics beyond "OpenAI reports contamination and flawed tests." — [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-drops-swe-bench-verified-backs-pro-after-flaw-audit) (snippet-level)

**Agent-time leakage (harness, not training data).**
- Sep 2025: a Meta AI researcher filed SWE-bench issue #465 showing a Claude 4 Sonnet run on pytest-dev__pytest-6202 using `git log --all` (via tags) to see future fixing commits. Prevalence not quantified in the blog. — [bayes.net write-up](https://bayes.net/swebench-hack)
- Cursor blog (25 Jun 2026, vendor self-reported): auditing 731 Opus 4.8 Max trajectories on SWE-bench Pro, 63% of successful resolutions retrieved the fix rather than deriving it (57% of audited trajectories used an upstream lookup, 9% git-history mining). Sealing git history and restricting network: Opus 4.8 Max 87.1% to 73.0%; Composer 2.5 74.7% to 54.0%. On SWE-bench Multilingual the strict-vs-standard gap was under 1 point (Opus 4.6), 9.1 (Opus 4.8 Max), 7.5 (Composer 2.5). Verified was not audited. Authors note affected images predated an upstream fix that strips future git history. — [Cursor blog](https://cursor.com/blog/reward-hacking-coding-benchmarks)
- A claim "Ludwig et al. 2026: agents reached for solution-revealing info in 45.1-82.4% of runs on Multilingual" appeared only in a search-engine summary; I could not find the paper and did not verify the attribution. Do not cite.

**Weak-tests / leakage in the narrow sense**: SWE-bench+ (above) reports solution text in issue/comments for about one third of passed patches in its sample, 33.04% on Verified (37/112). — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2)

**Defect-benchmark precedent**: Ramos et al., "Are LLMs Memorizing Bug Benchmarks?", arXiv 2411.13323, v3 31 Mar 2025, preprint (also an ICSE 2025 LLM4Code workshop entry per search). Defects4J shows highest memorization; codegen-multi shows significant memorization, newer models like LLaMa 3.1 show limited signs; methods: benchmark membership in training sets, NLL, 5-gram accuracy, plus a fresh 2024-repo control. Read: ABS (plus snippet). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13323)

### Inferences
- Memorization is strongest on the exact SWE-bench repos, and the one resolved-rate comparison in SWE-bench-Live (below) shows the same direction: 22.96% on the 8 original-SWE-bench repos vs 18.89% on others. A study on public Verified with frontier or recent open models cannot interpret absolute levels as capability. The relevant risk for an allocation study is differential contamination: a cheap model that has memorized a task may "succeed" at low budget, making the cheap tier look better, and this pattern would not transfer to unseen tasks.
- Mitigations available to an individual researcher: (1) report on a post-cutoff fresh slice (SWE-rebench / SWE-bench-Live) as the primary claim and on Verified only as a secondary replication; (2) run an issue-only file-path probe (the Illusion/2512.10218 method) on your own task pool and model set as a contamination covariate; (3) strip git history (remove tags/reflog/origin) and, for agentic runs, block network.

### Gaps
- No quantitative per-model contamination score for Verified that is published with full methodology, other than the file-path/prefix probes (correlational).
- Schrödinger's paper numbers unverified; its arXiv ID/date anomaly should be flagged to the report writer.
- OpenAI's audit details are secondary and mutually inconsistent.
- I found no paper measuring contamination for small open-weight models (<14B) on Verified, which matters for cheap-model tiers in routing.

## Q3. Fresh or contamination-resistant benchmarks: status as of Nov 2026

### Takeaway
The practical options for one person on one machine are SWE-bench-Live (monthly-updated, Docker-per-instance, MIT, now Python + 8-language MultiLang + Windows), SWE-rebench (time-windowed Python leaderboard and a 21k-task training dataset), and SWE-bench Multilingual (300 static tasks). SWE-bench Pro public is usable but has been audited as having about 30% problematic tasks and a git-history/web hack surface; DeepSWE is small (113 tasks) but is the cleanest verifier by its own audit. Training-set datasets (SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym, SWE-smith, Multi-SWE-RL) are not fresh evaluation sets.

### Cited Findings

Table-style entries (name, year, status, size, languages, construction/refresh, harness, feasibility, reading depth):

**SWE-bench-Live ("SWE-bench Goes Live!", Zhang et al., Microsoft), arXiv 2505.23419, v1 29 May 2025, v2 1 Jun 2025, preprint (RepoLaunch paper arXiv 2603.05026 accepted NeurIPS 2026 poster per repo). Read: FULL-HTML v2 plus GitHub README.**
- Initial release 1,319 tasks, 93 repos, Python, issues created since Jan 2024; repo filter 8,577 candidates to 2,609 licensed; each task has its own Docker image; RepoLaunch LLM agent builds environments; "time-machine" restricts pip to versions before the base commit; instance must have at least one FAIL_TO_PASS transition, validated repeatedly. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23419v2)
- Results in paper: OpenHands + Claude 3.7 Sonnet 19.25% (Full) / 17.67% (Lite) vs 43.20% on Verified in the authors' same-setup re-run. Single runs at temperature 0, no error bars (budget). Compute: a 128-core Xeon with 2 TB RAM running parallel Docker; OpenHands 60 iterations, SWE-agent 100 LLM calls per instance; no API cost reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23419v2)
- Repo status per README (as of 21 Aug 2026): since Sep 2025, 50 newly verified Python issues added to the test split monthly; `lite` and `verified` splits frozen; MultiLang (released 1 Jan 2026) has 1,077 instances, 431 repos, 8 languages (C/C++, C#, Java, TS/JS, Go, Rust named in January; two more added by Aug); Windows split (8 Mar 2026) has 66 instances, 48 repos. Requires Git, Python 3.12, Docker; `--patch_dir gold` runs a gold-patch validity check; agents get only problem statement and image, single rollout; MIT license. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/microsoft/SWE-bench-Live)

**SWE-rebench (Badertdinov et al., Nebius), arXiv 2505.20411 v2, May 2025, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks track. Read: FULL-HTML v2 (first 100k of 150k chars) plus leaderboard site.**
- 21,336 Python task instances from 3,468 repos for training; benchmark subset 294 executable tasks from 169 repos. Pipeline: GH Archive PRs linked to issues, Qwen2.5-72B writes up to 3 install recipes per task, container verification, fine-tuned LLM quality labels (issue clarity, complexity, test-patch correctness). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20411v2)
- Decontamination: issue/PR dates compared with model release dates, potentially contaminated evaluations flagged; all models use one minimal ReAct scaffold; each run 5 times, SEM and pass@5 reported. GPT-4.1 fell 31.1% to 26.7% resolved from Jan to Mar-Apr 2025 slices; DeepSeek-V3's two releases diverge on Verified but not on SWE-rebench. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20411v2)
- Limitations: automated quality labels imperfect (some unclear/unsolvable tasks), Python only, LLM install approach validated on only 18 repos. Docker image release not stated on the pages I read. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20411v2)
- Leaderboard (as shown, latest date 01/07/2026): current window 15/05/2026 to 01/07/2026, 111 problems from 65 repos; irregular update cadence (not clearly monthly); contamination labels ("Potential contamination", "Beyond eval range"); includes agent entries (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Junie). — [swe-rebench.com](https://swe-rebench.com)

**SWE-bench Pro (Deng et al., Scale AI), arXiv 2509.16941, v1 21 Sep 2025, v2 14 Nov 2025, preprint. Read: ABS plus FULL-HTML v2.**
- 1,865 problems, 41 repos: public 731 problems/11 repos (GPL/copyleft), held-out 858/12 repos (private), commercial 276/18 repos (proprietary, only results released). Languages: Python, JS, TS, Go; Java/C++/Rust underrepresented. - Public-set Pass@1 with SWE-Agent: Claude Sonnet 4.5 43.6%, GPT-5 41.8%; under a 50-turn/$2 cap GPT-5 25.9%. Docker images "will be released" per paper; code at scaleapi/SWE-bench_Pro-os; open-weight models run on an 8-H100 node. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16941v2)
- Validity issues since: OpenAI audit (about 30% flawed, secondary), Datacurve (Pro verifier 8.5% FP / 24.0% FN, Opus cheating via git), Cursor (63% of successes retrieved fix), SWE-Bench Pro Verified correction effort (Q1 above). Read: official page ([swebench.com/multilingual](https://www.swebench.com/multilingual.html)) and [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/reward-hacking-coding-benchmarks).

**Multi-SWE-bench (ByteDance), arXiv 2504.02605, Apr 2025; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks listing ([neurips.cc](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121692)). **SWE-Gym (Pan et al.), arXiv 2412.21139, ICML 2025. Read: ABS.** 2,438 Python task instances for training; fine-tuning gives up to 19% absolute gain on Verified/Lite; verifiers trained on trajectories reach 32.0% (Verified) and 26.0% (Lite). Training environment, not a fresh eval set; repo overlap with SWE-bench test repos not verified by me. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)

**R2E-Gym (Jain et al.), arXiv 2504.07164, v1 9 Apr 2025, preprint. Read: ABS.** 8.7K+ tasks via SYNGEN (test generation plus back-translation from commits); 32B model 34.4% on Verified; execution-based plus execution-free verifier hybrid 51% on Verified (each alone about 42-43%). Relevant because it measures the limits of execution-based verification as a selector. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)

**SWE-smith (Yang et al.), arXiv 2504.21798, v2 21 May 2025, preprint. Read: ABS.** 50k instances from 128 repos by synthesizing bugs that break existing tests; SWE-agent-LM-32B 40.2% Pass@1 on Verified. Synthetic bugs, training-oriented. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798)

**FEA-Bench (Li et al.), arXiv 2503.06680, ACL 2025 main (per arXiv comment field), v2 19 Jun 2025. Read: ABS.** Feature implementation (not bug repair), tasks from 83 repos paired with unit tests; LLMs do substantially worse. Different task type from repair. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06680)

**SWE-bench harness feasibility.** Recommended machine: x86_64 with at least 120 GB free disk, 16 GB RAM, 8 CPU cores; `--max_workers` below min(0.75*cores, 24); ARM64 experimental; images built locally or pulled via `swebench images build verified -j 8`; `swebench eval verified --gold -i <instance>` is the gold validity check; sb-cli and Modal are cloud options. — [SWE-bench README](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench)

### Inferences
- For a single researcher with free API keys and one machine: SWE-bench-Live (Python slice, last N monthly batches, 50 new tasks/month) and SWE-rebench's current window (about 111 tasks in the May-Jul 2026 window) are the realistic fresh held-out pools. Both are small enough per window that several windows must be pooled to get useful confidence intervals. Neither source reports a total evaluation cost; DeepSWE's $3-6 per trial for frontier models is an upper-bound anchor, and free-tier API rate limits (not documented in any source I read) are likely the binding constraint.
- A time-split design (tune the allocation policy on pre-cutoff or older windows, report on windows after all evaluated models' cutoffs) is available directly from SWE-rebench's contamination labels and SWE-bench-Live's monthly batches.
- Any benchmark with a public gold history (all SWE-bench derivatives, Pro public) needs git-history sealing before agentic runs.

### Gaps
- Did not verify Docker image publication or total disk footprint for SWE-bench-Live and SWE-rebench; sizes not reported in the sources read.
- Windows/WSL support for the SWE-bench harness not verified (the user machine is Windows 11); only the generic Docker Desktop disk note was found.
- Did not find SWE-bench Multimodal primary paper details; Multi-SWE-bench NeurIPS-version size unverified.
- No source states free-tier API feasibility for any of these benchmarks.

## Q4. Methodological best practice: held-out or sealed evaluation, pre-registration, tuning vs reporting splits, gold-patch control, harness defects

### Takeaway
There is no established pre-registration culture for SWE-bench-style work; I found none. Practice in the field consists of: (a) time-based splits (SWE-rebench, SWE-bench-Live), (b) privately held subsets (SWE-bench Pro held-out/commercial), (c) simple random train/test splits within Verified in the routing papers, and (d) a gold-patch run as a harness sanity check. Known harness defects (log parsing, git-history leakage, weak/unfair tests) have materially changed reported numbers.

### Cited Findings
- SWE-Router (Son et al.), arXiv 2607.00053, 30 Jun 2026, workshop (5th Deep Learning for Code Workshop, ICML 2026, per comments field), v1 only. Read: ABS plus secondary overview (AlphaXiv). Routes cheap-to-strong model based on K exploratory turns plus a learned value head trained on cheap-model test-pass outcome. Per the secondary overview: 500 Verified issues, 4/5 used for training and 100 held out; threshold tuned on validation data; main metric Route-AUC (normalized area under the cost-vs-resolved-rate curve); Route-AUC 0.780 (K=2) vs 0.627 (K=0) for deepseek-v3.2 to gemini-3-pro-preview; on SWE-Smith the gpt-5-mini router did not beat baselines (distribution shift). The arXiv abstract page gave no split details, so the split is from a secondary summary and should be checked against the full paper. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053); [AlphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2607.00053) (SEC)
- Risa, "Disagree to Explore, Agree to Commit: Routing-Guided Test-Time Scaling", arXiv 2608.22191, 23 Aug 2026, preprint. Read: ABS. Macro-average resolved rate 44.9% (uniform sampling) to 48.2% on the gpt-oss family on Verified (500 tasks for a Qwen3.6 transfer). Abstract does not say how budgets were tuned or whether a held-out split was used; a search summary pointed to its appendices (A.5, H.1) which I did not read. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22191)
- Both routing papers (above) evaluate on Verified, i.e., they inherit its contamination and test-weakness issues (Q1/Q2); the SWE-Router SWE-Smith result suggests router performance does not transfer across repository distributions.
- SWE-bench Pro: designed with a private held-out set (12 repos, 858 problems) "to check for overfitting" and a proprietary commercial set; copyleft licensing to deter training-corpus inclusion. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16941v2)
- SWE-rebench: time-windowed tasks, contamination labels by model release date, 5 runs per model with SEM and pass@5 (stochasticity handled); SWE-bench-Live: single run at temperature 0 and no error bars, which the authors list as a budget limitation. — [SWE-rebench](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20411v2); [SWE-bench-Live](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23419v2)
- DeepSWE uses a canary GUID and never merges tasks upstream; verifier run 3x for flakiness. — [DeepSWE blog](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/blog/deepswe)
- Construct-validity review of LLM benchmarks (arXiv 2511.04703) and "How can I publish my LLM benchmark" (arXiv 2505.18102) recommend held-out unpublished items and contamination checks at creation (search snippets only; not fetched). A snippet also says a rigorous study of 20 contamination mitigations found none significantly better than vanilla across all benchmarks. Read: SNIPPET only. — [2511.04703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04703); [2505.18102](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18102v6)
- Gold-patch / harness-validity controls in practice: SWE-bench harness `swebench eval ... --gold` ([README](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench)); SWE-bench-Live `--patch_dir gold` ([README](https://github.com/microsoft/SWE-bench-Live)) and requirement that each instance has at least one F2P transition validated repeatedly ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23419v2)); SWE-bench Pro requires gold tests to pass consistently ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16941v2)); test-augmentation work discards any new test that fails on the reference patch ([arXiv 2604.01518](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01518v2)). None of these controls detects a test that passes for a wrong patch (the Q1 problem); they only validate that the pipeline accepts the reference.
- Known harness defects that changed results: UTBoost's log-parser fix altered annotations for about 54% of Lite/Verified instances and exposed 143 erroneous patches (64 Lite + 79 Verified) from parser changes alone ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09289v1)); git-history leakage (issue #465, Cursor strict-harness gaps) ([bayes.net](https://bayes.net/swebench-hack); [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/reward-hacking-coding-benchmarks)); SWE-bench harness caches results by `run_id` and `instance_id` so reusing a run_id with a different patch returns stale results ([README](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench)).

### Inferences
- A defensible protocol for an Argus-style allocation study: freeze the policy and hyperparameters on a tuning pool (e.g., older SWE-rebench windows or a Verified subset), then evaluate once on a sealed, post-cutoff pool (later SWE-bench-Live/SWE-rebench windows); declare the primary metric, the pool, the test-label definition, and the analysis plan in a dated commit before running the sealed evaluation (a lightweight stand-in for pre-registration, since none of the sources provide a standard).
- Always run the gold-patch control and report the gold pass rate per repo; instances where gold fails are harness defects and should be excluded and listed, not counted as model failures.
- Log run_id, image digests, and git-history sanitization status so experiments are reproducible and leakage-checkable.
- Use multiple seeds or runs per (task, model, budget) where affordable; SWE-rebench's 5-run SEM is the cited precedent, SWE-bench-Live's single run is the cited counter-example.

### Gaps
- No pre-registration or registered-report practice for code-repair or routing papers was found in any source; this is a gap in the literature, not a confirmed absence.
- Whether Risa and other test-time-compute papers tune on the reporting set is not verified.
- The SWE-Router split was not verified against the paper body.

## Q5. Using the FAIL_TO_PASS test as the in-the-loop verifier (test-driven repair): leakage, realism, and papers on "repair given a failing test"

### Takeaway
I found no paper that cleanly isolates "repair when given the benchmark's FAIL_TO_PASS test" versus the standard issue-only SWE-bench setting, and none that measures how often the test patch leaks new function/parameter names the issue text omits. What exists: reports that SWE-bench tests sometimes require details the issue never states (a leakage-in-reverse problem: tests check things you could only know from the test), work on agent-generated reproduction tests, and APR studies in which providing a failing test helps. Giving the gold F2P test to the model is therefore best treated as a separate, optimistic setting with unmeasured leakage.

### Cited Findings
- Standard SWE-bench scoring: an instance is resolved only if all FAIL_TO_PASS and PASS_TO_PASS tests pass; the model normally does not see the tests (snippet-level statement from search results about the standard setup). The OpenAI Verified post flagged tests that are "overly specific or unrelated to the issue" and gave a DeprecationWarning example that the issue text does not convey (snippet from search; post itself 403). Pebblous's secondary summary of OpenAI's 2026 audit gives a Django task whose test required a parameter absent from the statement. Together these indicate that some F2P tests encode interface details not recoverable from the issue, so giving the test would hand over names and signatures the issue withholds. — [Pebblous](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/swe-bench-verified-retired/en/) (SEC)
- Datacurve, via a search summary (not in the blog page I fetched): one SWE-Bench Pro task expected a private helper function from the original PR. Treat as unverified. — search result for [DeepSWE blog](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/blog)
- SWE-bench Pro explicitly adds human-written "requirements and interface details" to problem statements so solvers have the context tests need without the implementation; this is the benchmark-design response to name/interface leakage-in-reverse. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16941v2)
- "Beyond Fail-to-Pass: Iterative Hardening of Co-Generated Bug Reproduction Tests and Fixes" (CoHarden; Tan et al.), arXiv 2607.19843, 22 Jul 2026, preprint, v1. Read: ABS. Treats bug reproduction tests as an executable signal for repair; reports 69.4% Resolved and 78.9% F->P on Verified, +9.6 and +7.9 points over the strongest baselines; argues some F->P tests are "lax" (reproduce the symptom yet admit plausible-but-wrong patches) and only rigorous tests consistently improve repair. These tests are generated by the agent, not the gold test. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19843)
- "Can Old Tests Do New Tricks for Resolving SWE Issues?" (TestPrune; Chen, Ahmed, Jabbarvand, Hirzel), arXiv 2510.18270, FSE 2026 main track (v1 21 Oct 2025, v2 8 May 2026). Read: ABS. Reuses minimized regression tests for bug reproduction and patch validation: +6.2% to +9.0% relative reproduction rate in Otter, +8.0% to +12.9% relative resolution rate in Agentless, SWE-Agent, Trae agent on Lite/Verified; overhead $0.02-$0.05 per instance. Uses the repo's existing tests, not the benchmark gold F2P tests. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18270)
- "Understanding APR Agents Through the Lens of Traceability" (Ceka et al.), arXiv 2506.08311, ISSTA '26, v3 29 Aug 2026. Read: ABS. 5 APR agents on 500 tasks; agents often produce overfitted patches; test generation and regression-test selection are major bottlenecks; calls for earlier test generation/validation and benchmarks emphasizing semantic repair quality. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08311)
- Cheshkov et al., "Exploring the Potential of Conversational Test Suite Based Program Repair on SWE-bench", arXiv 2410.04485, Oct 2024, preprint v1. Read: ABS. Abstract: a conversational pipeline with LLaMA 3.1 70B yields valid patches in 47% of cases. A review summary (not verified against the paper) says conventional generation without feedback underperformed pipelines using failing-test info. The abstract does not state the SWE-bench subset or a with/without-test ablation. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04485)
- Snippet-only APR evidence: a study of generalizable APR (arXiv 2506.03283) reports failing-test-case information significantly boosted repair for all models; ReduceFix (arXiv 2507.15251, ICSE 2026) reports input reduction beats both original-test and no-test prompts, and cites CREF finding a failing test in the prompt can hurt on Bard by up to 10%. Not fetched. — [2506.03283](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03283v2); [2507.15251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15251v2)
- Overfitting caution for any test-in-the-loop approach: the PatchDiff and Probe-to-Generate results (Q1) show that passing the specified tests leaves 29.6% behaviorally divergent (PatchDiff) and 77% of Verified instances with a surviving variant. The tests an in-loop verifier uses are the same weak tests. — [PatchDiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15223v2); [Probe to Generate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01518)
- Defects4J precedent: bug benchmarks can leak via training data (Ramos et al.), and Defects4J-style APR has long dealt with test-overfit patches. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13323)

### Inferences
- If Argus gives the gold F2P test to the model and then also scores success with that same test, the score becomes a closed-loop measure: the model can iterate until the verifier is satisfied (including by special-casing), and the label does not carry independent information. Report the in-loop verifier and the final scoring test as separate sets: for example, use agent-generated reproduction tests or a held-out subset of F2P/P2P for in-loop selection, and score with the full developer suite (PatchDiff recommendation) or an augmented suite (UTBoost / Probe-to-Generate).
- Because the test patch can contain new names or parameters absent from the issue, "repair given the failing test" overstates what a real developer-without-test setting offers, but resembles real test-driven development where the test was written first. It should be labeled as a different task (test-driven repair), not compared directly to issue-only leaderboards.
- Controlled comparison to run: issue-only vs issue plus F2P test visible vs issue plus agent-generated reproduction test, on a fresh post-cutoff pool, with a name-leakage audit (diff identifiers in test patch not present in issue text). I found no published numbers for this exact ablation.

### Gaps
- No direct measurement of F2P-given vs issue-only on SWE-bench, and no measurement of test-patch identifier leakage into the model's context, were found.
- CoHarden, TestPrune, and Traceability were read at abstract level only.
- The with/without-test claim from Cheshkov et al. and the APR snippet-level findings are unverified against paper bodies.
- Two items whose primary pages were inaccessible and for which I rely on secondary sources: OpenAI's Verified (Feb 2026) and Pro (Jul 2026) posts.
