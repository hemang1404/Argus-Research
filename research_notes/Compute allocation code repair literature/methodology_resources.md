## 1. How routing / stopping / test-time-compute studies build an "arm library" and evaluate by replay; validity issues

### Takeaway
Every credible study here builds a full-feedback table (each task x each model/strategy, ideally multiple draws) once, then evaluates routers or stoppers by looking up stored outcomes and costs instead of re-running inference. The main validity hazards that the papers themselves document are feedback-conditioned or post-hoc-chosen arms, concentration of the effect in a few tasks, tuning/evaluation overlap, simulated or imperfect quality estimators, and (for logged rather than full-feedback data) selection bias. Only a minority of these papers use multiple samples per arm or report task-clustered uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
**Arm-library construction and replay, paper by paper**
- RouterBench (Hu et al., arXiv 2403.12031, Mar 2024, arXiv preprint, no venue listed; FULL). "405,467 samples ... covering 11 models, 8 datasets, and 64 tasks" (Sec. 4.3 also says 14 LLMs including 3 RAG-specific, and Sec. 6 says "seven datasets": internal inconsistencies). Each stored sample has quality and a cost field; costs come from provider pricing, with open models priced via Together AI. Routers are tested by replaying stored outputs ("can be efficiently tested without inference"). Quality is exact match for 5 datasets and GPT-4-judged for MBPP, MT-Bench and RAG. Predictive routers were trained on 70% and tested on 30% of each task. — [RouterBench html](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- RouterBench limitations stated by the authors: only performance and cost (no latency); the cascade router assumes a judge with "perfect knowledge" simulated via an error parameter; "none of the routing algorithms significantly outperform the baseline Zero router" among predictive routers. One draw per prompt-model pair; no seeds. — [RouterBench html](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- SPROUT / CARROT (Somerstep et al., arXiv 2502.03261, Feb 2025, v2 May 2025, preprint; FULL for first ~100k of 172k characters). SPROUT has 44,241 prompts (30,968 train / 6,636 val / 6,637 test) with responses from 14 models in Table 2 (text says both 14 and M=15 in different places; Causal-routing paper says 13). Responses are zero-shot, graded by Llama-3.1-70B against a gold answer (MixEval protocol), with per-model input/output token counts from each tokenizer and costs per 1M tokens. One response per prompt-model pair. The visible text states no seeds, no confidence intervals and no cost-accuracy AUC for SPROUT (the unread 72k characters may contain them). — [CARROT html](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03261v2)
- RouteLLM (Ong et al., arXiv 2406.18665, Jun 2024, v4 Feb 2025, preprint; FULL v4). Trains binary strong/weak routers on ~65k Chatbot Arena pairwise comparisons (from ~80k battles, across 64 models; 5k held out for validation), augmented with ~1,500 golden-label MMLU validation questions and ~120K LLM-judge-labelled samples (cost "around $700"). Evaluates on MMLU (14,042 questions, 5-shot), MT-Bench (160 questions, GPT-4 judge) and GSM8K (>1,000 problems, 8-shot) with GPT-4-1106-preview (strong) vs Mixtral 8x7B (weak). A decontamination check at 0.95 embedding similarity removed overlapping eval prompts. Metrics: CPT(x%) (call-performance threshold: share of strong-model calls needed to recover x% of the performance gap) and APGR (area under the performance-vs-cost curve). Baseline: random router under the same cost constraint. Best cost reductions vs GPT-4 (Table 6): MT-Bench 3.66x at CPT(50%) and 2.49x at CPT(80%); MMLU 1.41x / 1.14x; GSM8K 1.49x / 1.27x. — [RouteLLM html v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665v4)
- Cascade routing (Dekoninck, Baader, Vechev, "A Unified Approach to Routing and Cascading for LLMs", arXiv 2410.10347; v1 Oct 2024, v5 revised 26 Oct 2026; preprint; FULL, all 150k characters). Benchmarks: RouterBench (3, 5 and 11 models, with Gaussian noise added to quality and cost estimates to emulate estimator error), SWE-bench Verified (10 leaderboard submissions' logs; post-hoc quality known, cost estimated from latency), Minerva Math + LiveCodeBench (Qwen2.5-Coder/Math 7B and 1.5B), and classification/open-form sets with Llama-3.1 8B/70B/405B, Gemma 2B/9B/27B, Mistral 7B/8x7B/8x22B. Metric: AUC of the quality-cost curve across budgets from cheapest to most expensive model (following RouterBench). Results: RouterBench gains of 1-4% over baselines (up to 8% over cascading and 12% over routing in the 5-model noise sweep); SWE-bench AUC 54.12 for cascade routing vs 40.47 routing and 38.52 baseline cascade (claimed up to 14% gain); Math+Code 75.5 / 64.8 (about 11% over baseline cascade); gains shrink to "up to 1.2%" when quality estimation is poor. Quality-estimator quality is the stated key success factor. — [Cascade routing html](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- Cascade routing replay protocol details (appendix): 5% of RouterBench (~2,000 samples) used for hyperparameter tuning and the remaining 95% for evaluation; SWE-bench Verified and LiveCodeBench/Minerva are split 50/50 into training and calibration subsets (sample counts not stated in the text I retrieved); poor-estimation experiments split each dataset 50/50 train/test and then split the training half again 50/50 between estimator training and validation. Confidence intervals: bootstrap over dataset samples on the AUC difference vs each baseline; "significantly better" means the difference is positive and its 2-sigma interval excludes zero. — [Cascade routing html](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- SWE-Router (Son, Yoon, Tang, Wang, Wolf, Bogunovic, arXiv 2607.00053, 30 Jun 2026, 5th Deep Learning for Code Workshop at ICML 2026; abstract page read plus HTML body via summarizer). A weak model runs a few exploratory turns; a value head (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, 2-class head, LoRA r=32, alpha=64, 5 epochs) reads the partial trajectory and decides whether to escalate. Agent scaffold: mini-SWE-agent with a 75-step budget. Weak models gpt-5-mini and deepseek-v3.2; strong model gemini-3-pro-preview (two weak/strong pairs). Table 1 (instances / resolved % / cost): SWE-Smith train 1,692 / 33.5% / $70.1 (gpt-5-mini) and 1,755 / 31.9% / $85.7 (deepseek-v3.2); SWE-Smith val 203, 210, 210 instances (gemini-3-pro: $121.3); SWE-Smith test 339 / 346 / 173 instances (gemini-3-pro: $95.6); SWE-bench Verified 500 instances each: 55.6% resolved / $16.6 (gpt-5-mini), 62.4% / $22.6 (deepseek-v3.2), 70.4% / $391.3 (gemini-3-pro). Totals: $105.4, $130.5, $608.2. One trajectory per instance per model; no seeds; no confidence intervals reported. Training used SWE-Smith train plus 4/5 of a 5-fold split of SWE-bench Verified, and the paper states the "genuinely held-out slice consists of 100 instances". The random-assignment reference band uses 400 Monte-Carlo permutations. No oracle baseline is mentioned. The text I retrieved does not explicitly describe replay, but per-instance stored outcomes and costs imply it. — [SWE-Router abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053); [SWE-Router html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- SWE-Router results and internal inconsistencies: SWE-bench Verified, deepseek-v3.2 -> gemini-3-pro, SWE-Router (K=2) Route-AUC 0.780 vs 0.627 for the non-temporal (K=0) router (+15.3 pp). For gpt-5-mini -> gemini-3-pro, best Route-AUC 0.709 (K=4); the text says "+12 pp" over the strongest baseline while the table implies about +14 pp over Embed+LR (0.565): unexplained discrepancy. On SWE-Smith test the gpt-5-mini router did not beat baselines (attributed to repository-split distribution shift), and AUROC of the value head does not track Route-AUC consistently. — [SWE-Router html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- "Resample or Reroute?" (Teng-Ruei Chen, arXiv 2607.08665, v1 9 Jul 2026, v3 2 Sep 2026, cs.LG, single author, preprint, 33 pages incl. 14-page supplement; abstract page read plus ~200k of 203,756 characters of HTML body). Fresh primary experiment: MBPP+ v0.2.0 (EvalPlus 0.3.1), 378 queries pinned by SHA-256, 152 TEST queries by hash-defined outer split; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-AWQ; D=10 stochastic draws per query per model (152 x 10 x 2 = 3,040 candidates) plus a greedy candidate kept as provenance only. 1,520 audited episodes (1,240 observable stops, 280 verifier rejections, 198 evaluator-only false positives). Results: 14B false-positive stops leave +2.592 pp of 7B recovery (95% CI [+1.618, +3.664]); fixed reroute to 14B beats leave-one-out 7B resampling by +2.882 pts ([+0.931, +5.201]). Other banks: LiveCodeBench 136 FIT / 68 DEV / 137 sealed TEST queries (70 eligible); BigCodeBench 483 FIT / 211 DEV / 405 TEST, up to 3 sequential Qwen14 history attempts per episode. — [Resample or Reroute abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08665); [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Resample-or-Reroute uncertainty method: deterministic 10,000-replicate percentile bootstrap that resamples query clusters, "never individual draws"; Bonferroni-corrected 97.5% two-sided intervals for model-family extensions; 20,000 PCG64 query-cluster replicates for one-sided studentized max-T bounds on LiveCodeBench; one-sided exact Clopper-Pearson lower bound for prevalence; 10^6 within-episode label permutations for the exchangeable reference. — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Test-time compute / sampling studies: Large Language Monkeys (Brown et al., arXiv 2407.21787, Jul 2024, v3 Dec 2024, preprint; ABSTRACT only) defines coverage (fraction of problems solved by any sample), shows log-linear-ish coverage growth over four orders of magnitude of samples, and on SWE-bench Lite with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct goes from 15.9% (1 sample) to 56% (250 samples) vs a stated single-sample SOTA of 43%; warns majority vote and reward models plateau after several hundred samples where no verifier exists. — [LLM Monkeys abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)
- Snell et al., "Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters" (arXiv 2408.03314, Aug 2024, preprint; ABSTRACT only): per-prompt compute-optimal allocation by difficulty; >4x efficiency over best-of-N; a smaller model with test-time compute can beat a 14x larger model in a FLOPs-matched comparison on problems where the small model has non-trivial success. — [Snell abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)
- AutoMix (arXiv 2310.12963, Oct 2023, v5 Jan 2025, NeurIPS 2024 per the arXiv comments; ABSTRACT only): few-shot self-verification plus POMDP router; five LMs, five datasets; reports "reducing computational cost by over 50% for comparable performance". — [AutoMix abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12963)
- FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, arXiv 2305.05176, May 2023, preprint; ABSTRACT only): LLM cascade; up to 98% cost reduction at GPT-4 parity or +4% accuracy at equal cost; API fees can differ by two orders of magnitude. — [FrugalGPT abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)
- RouterArena (ICLR 2026) and RouterEval (200M+ performance records from 8,500+ LLMs across 12 benchmarks): SNIPPET only (search-result summaries); not fetched. — [RouterArena html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00202) ; RouterEval described via third-party review only.

**Validity issues documented in the literature**
- Feedback-conditioned / post-hoc arms: in Resample-or-Reroute the Llama and Nemotron arms "were specified after the Qwen14-to-Qwen7 outcome was known", so they are post-outcome robustness checks, not independent replications. Its legacy 11-model replay (GSM8K, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond, HumanEval+) is kept only to document why claims were removed: fixed-bank permutations "are not independent datasets", parameter-count costs "are not prices", and its inspected split "is not confirmatory evidence". — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Selection / identification: stopping debt was identified using hidden correctness; whether it is predictable from legal runtime information is untested. "The three gates reduce overclaiming but do not create population identification from finite banks." — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Concentration: only 28 of 152 query clusters contribute nonzero mass and the top ten account for 60.2% of it. — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Tuning/leakage and evaluator drift: the matched-information audit ran on an already-opened TEST bank, the sealed-TEST opening was self-authorized, and the evaluator was modified after an invalidated opening without freezing the final evaluator source. Adding models after a failed gate "counts as a new study"; extending a failed FIT bank requires "a new preregistered generation, not an outcome-conditioned extension". — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Cost measures are not decision-grade in that paper: costs are descriptive and omit prefill, verifier, queueing, dollar and energy costs; no scalar exchange rate across calls, tokens, latency and model size; BigCodeBench FIT reroute averaged 339.710 generated tokens per draw vs 509.705 for resample, wall time 3.899 s vs 9.129 s per draw (measured in separate runs). — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Training/evaluation overlap in SWE-Router: 4/5 of SWE-bench Verified is used for training the value head, leaving 100 held-out instances; only two model pairs, one backbone, no seeds, no CIs, and inconsistent results across splits (stated above). — [SWE-Router html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- Simulated rather than measured estimators: cascade-routing's RouterBench experiments add Gaussian noise to quality and cost, and its SWE-bench cost is a latency regression, so results depend on estimator accuracy (stated by the authors). — [Cascade routing html](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- Logged (non-full-feedback) data are biased: "Causal LLM Routing" (arXiv 2505.16037, 2025, preprint; FULL) notes logged data reveal only the deployed model's outcome; with RouterBench (35,712 prompts, 11 models) and SPROUT (44,241 prompts, 13 models; 80% train, 10/10 val/test) turned into simulated logs by sampling one model per prompt with probability proportional to exp(accuracy), an uncorrected baseline reaches 66.88 vs 85.99 for full-feedback on SPROUT-BERT at lambda=0 (58.83 vs 38.97 at lambda=1000), and doubly robust corrections recover "+10-15% routing accuracy at comparable cost". The summarizer flagged a conflicting Table 7 AUC (RM-Softmax 0.3320 above Full-Feedback 0.1727), so check that table before citing. — [Causal LLM Routing html](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16037)

### Inferences
- A full-feedback arm library (every task x every arm x several seeds) sidesteps the logged-data selection bias entirely; the cost is that it fixes the policy class to things whose outcomes can be looked up. Any policy whose later action depends on feedback from earlier actions (retry with test output, escalation after a failed verifier) needs the library to store the conditional continuation (the "history attempts" design in Resample-or-Reroute), otherwise the replay is not a valid counterfactual.
- None of RouterBench, SPROUT, RouteLLM or SWE-Router used multiple seeds per arm (SWE-Router: explicitly one trajectory per instance per model); Resample-or-Reroute (D=10) and Large Language Monkeys (up to 250+ samples) are the multi-draw exceptions. A study with 3 seeds per arm would already be better replicated than the repository-level routing papers.
- Tuning/test hygiene practices worth copying: hash-defined, pre-registered splits; sealed test set; frozen evaluator; cluster-by-task bootstrap; pre-registered support minima.

### Gaps
- RouterBench GitHub/HF dataset card, the SPROUT Hugging Face page and the SWE-Router released trajectory dataset were not opened, so released-artifact sizes and licenses beyond the paper text are unverified.
- The last ~3.7k characters of the Resample-or-Reroute HTML and the last ~72k characters of CARROT were not read.
- SWE-Router does not state (in the retrieved text) whether routing is evaluated by replay or by live re-execution.
- The RouterBench / Cascade-routing / CARROT papers' exact per-split sample counts for SWE-bench were not stated in the text retrieved.

## 2. Sample sizes, statistical power, CIs, detectable effects, minimum tasks

### Takeaway
Published routing studies are mostly under-powered or unreported on uncertainty: SWE-Router uses 500 instances (100 truly held-out) with no CIs, while the two papers with proper CIs (cascade routing; Resample-or-Reroute) use sample bootstrap or task-cluster bootstrap. Miller's evaluation-statistics paper gives the closed-form paired/clustered formulas; applying them, 300 tasks x 1 draw can detect roughly 5-8 percentage-point paired differences at 80% power, so 300 tasks supports only large effects. No paper states a minimum task count for learning a small-feature router.

### Cited Findings
**Numbers actually used in the papers**
- RouterBench: 405,467 samples (about 35.7k prompts x 11 models), 11 models, 8 datasets; predictive routers on 70/30 splits. — [RouterBench html](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031); prompt count from [Causal LLM Routing](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16037)
- SPROUT: 44,241 prompts, 14 models, 1 response each. — [CARROT html](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03261v2)
- RouteLLM: ~65k preference pairs for training; eval MMLU 14,042 / MT-Bench 160 / GSM8K >1,000. — [RouteLLM html](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665v4)
- Cascade routing: ~2,000 samples (5% of RouterBench) for tuning; 50/50 train/calibration on SWE-bench Verified (500 tasks nominally, counts not stated). — [Cascade routing html](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- SWE-Router: 500 SWE-bench Verified instances, 100 genuinely held-out; 1,692-1,755 SWE-Smith train instances; 1 trajectory per instance per model; 400 permutations for the random band; no CIs. — [SWE-Router html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- Resample-or-Reroute: 152 TEST queries x 10 draws x 2 models; effect +2.592 pp with 95% CI [+1.618, +3.664] (half-width about 1.0 pp); 28/152 query clusters carry nonzero mass; BigCodeBench support gate required at least 200 stopped queries and 400 stopped episodes, at least 25 episodes and 20 queries of each advantage sign (observed 23/19 and 22/19, so the gate failed and DEV/TEST were never opened); authors say these minima are "our preregistered evidence standard, not a natural constant" and that the paper gives no power calculation. — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- Large Language Monkeys: up to 250 samples per SWE-bench Lite issue, coverage 15.9% -> 56%. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)

**Statistical methods**
- Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals" (arXiv 2411.00640, Nov 2024, stat.AP, 14 pages; FULL via HTML): treat questions as a draw from a super-population; clustered SE (Eq. 4) can be up to about 3x the naive SE in his real-data example (DROP 1.34 vs 0.44); paired SE (Eq. 7) = sqrt(Var(s_A-B)/n) = sqrt(SE_A^2 + SE_B^2 - 2 SE_A SE_B Corr) so positive between-model correlation shrinks variance (correlation 0.5 with per-model variance 1/12 cuts the variance of the difference by a factor of 1/3); Table 5 examples: MATH diff +2.5% (SE 0.7%, corr 0.50), HumanEval -3.1% (SE 2.1%, corr 0.64), MGSM -2.7% (SE 1.7%, corr 0.37). — [Miller html](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640)
- Miller's power formulas: n = (z_{alpha/2} + z_beta)^2 (omega^2 + sigma_A^2/K_A + sigma_B^2/K_B) / delta^2, with omega^2 = Var(x_A) + Var(x_B) - 2 Cov(x_A, x_B), K = samples per question, delta = minimum detectable effect. Worked example: omega^2 = 1/9, sigma^2 = 0, delta = 0.03, 80% power, alpha = 0.05 gives n about 969 questions (paper suggests new evals have at least about 1,000). Second example: with sigma_A^2 = sigma_B^2 = 1/6, omega^2 = 1/9, n = 198, raising K from 1 to 10 cuts the MDE from 13.2% to 7.5%. Resampling cannot cut total variance by more than 2/3 (limit), and pooling K*N answers in one SE is invalid because repeats of the same question are not independent. He advises against lowering temperature to reduce variance. — [Miller html](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640)
- Cascade routing uses a bootstrap over dataset samples on the AUC difference (2-sigma criterion); Resample-or-Reroute uses a task-cluster percentile bootstrap with 10,000 replicates (details above). — [Cascade routing](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347); [Resample or Reroute](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)

### Inferences
- Derived (not from any paper): plugging Miller's own example variances (omega^2 = 1/9, sigma^2 = 0) into his MDE formula with alpha = 0.05 and power 80% gives MDE about 5.4 pp at n = 300 tasks, K = 1. With within-task sampling noise sigma_A^2 = sigma_B^2 = 1/6 and K = 3, MDE about 7.6 pp at n = 300. These are for a simple paired accuracy difference; a cost-quality frontier (AUC) difference is a different statistic and no paper provides its power formula, so it should be done by simulation/bootstrap from pilot data.
- Because effects in the code-repair routing literature are small when real (Resample-or-Reroute: about 2.6-2.9 pp; RouteLLM: 1.1-1.5x cost reduction on MMLU/GSM8K at CPT(50%)), a 300-task study is adequately powered only for effects of about 5+ pp or for within-pair comparisons with strong correlation; detecting 2-3 pp needs roughly 1,000 tasks (Miller's 969 figure for 3 pp at omega^2 = 1/9) or a much higher between-arm correlation.
- Extra seeds help only up to the 2/3 variance-reduction ceiling (Miller), so beyond about 3-6 seeds per arm, adding tasks is the better use of budget; tasks, not draws, are the unit of inference, so bootstrap/cluster at the task level.
- Learning a router: none of the papers state a minimum task count. Reference points: SWE-Router trained on thousands of trajectories (1,692+ SWE-Smith plus 400 Verified) with a 7B LoRA value head and still showed split-dependent failure; cascade routing tunes on about 2,000 samples. For a few-feature (low-dimensional) router, leave-one-task-out or nested CV over 300 tasks is feasible but the held-out folds will be small; treat any 300-task router result as exploratory and pre-register the held-out set.

### Gaps
- No source gave a rule or empirical study of the minimum number of tasks for a few-feature router; nothing found on power for frontier-AUC differences specifically.
- Per-model correlation of per-task pass outcomes on code repair (needed for omega^2) was not found; the example variances above are Miller's illustrative values, not code-repair measurements.

## 3. Baselines reviewers expect and metric definitions

### Takeaway
The recurring baseline set is: oracle (per-task best arm, cheapest on ties), best single model, random/zero router that mixes models along the frontier, kNN/embedding+LR/XGB prompt routers, RouteLLM binary routers, threshold cascades with a verifier/judge, AutoMix self-verification, and for agentic repair, partial-trajectory routing (SWE-Router). Area-under-curve metrics differ by paper (AIQ, APGR, Route-AUC, plain AUC), so a study should define and report one explicitly plus a fixed-budget operating-point comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Oracle: RouterBench's Oracle "represents the best possible router", always picking the best-performing LLM and the cheapest one on ties; it "achieves near-optimal performance at a low cost". — [RouterBench html](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- Zero router (RouterBench): a probabilistic mix of LLMs lying on the non-decreasing convex hull (NDCH) of the individual models' (cost, quality) points. AIQ (Average Improvement in Quality) = area under the NDCH normalized by (c_max - c_min). — [RouterBench html](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- RouterBench predictive baselines: kNN (best: 40 neighbors, all-MiniLM-L12-v2) and MLP (two 100-neuron hidden layers); cascading with a simulated judge error rate (performance degrades "rapidly" as judge error rises). — [RouterBench html](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031)
- RouteLLM: random router at the same cost constraint; metrics CPT(x%) and APGR (area under performance-vs-cost curve; "performance gap recovered" is relative to the weak/strong gap). — [RouteLLM html](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665v4)
- SPROUT baselines: Zero Router, RouteLLM MF and RouteLLM Roberta, Not Diamond RoRF, and the cost-unaware RouterBench router; headline claim that CARROT matches or exceeds GPT-4o at about 30% of cost on each benchmark. — [CARROT html](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03261v2)
- Cascade routing baselines: deterministic routing, threshold cascading, optimal cascading (Theorem 2), linear interpolation on the Pareto frontier (Hu et al.). FrugalGPT, AutoMix and RouteLLM are discussed only in related work and not run as baselines. — [Cascade routing html](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10347)
- SWE-Router baselines: Embed+LR, Embed+kNN, Embed+XGB on text-embedding-3-large, non-temporal K=0 router, and a random-assignment band from 400 permutations. Route-AUC is described as "normalized area under the cost vs. resolved-rate curve". — [SWE-Router html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- AutoMix is the standard self-verification + POMDP router baseline (ABSTRACT only). — [AutoMix abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12963)
- Best-of-n / repeated sampling with a verifier is the standard test-time-compute baseline; coverage (pass@k) is an upper bound and verifier-selected performance can fall short; majority voting and reward models plateau after several hundred samples. — [LLM Monkeys abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787); [Snell abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)
- Resample-vs-reroute baselines: fixed escalation to the larger model, leave-one-out resampling of the smaller model; the paper explicitly frames the comparison as "fixed-action ranking, not a conditional selector". — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- RouterArena (ICLR 2026) scores routers on accuracy, cost, routing optimality (cheapest correct selection) and robustness to query perturbations (SNIPPET only). — [RouterArena html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00202)

### Inferences
- Reviewer-expected minimum set for code repair: (1) each single model on the Pareto set, (2) best single model at matched cost, (3) oracle (per-task cheapest-correct), (4) random/zero router interpolated along the convex hull, (5) a prompt-feature router (embedding or hand-crafted features, LR/kNN/XGB), (6) a fixed cascade small -> large with the same verifier, (7) best-of-n / resample-with-verifier at the same spend, (8) AutoMix-style self-verification gate, (9) partial-trajectory routing where agentic. Report a matched-budget operating point and an AUC; define whether the AUC is normalized by cost range (AIQ/Route-AUC) or by gap recovered (APGR).

### Gaps
- The exact formula for SWE-Router's Route-AUC normalization and its treatment of cost endpoints was not retrieved beyond the one-line description.
- Not checked whether Route-AUC, AIQ and APGR are mutually comparable numerically (they are not defined identically).

## 4. Model price spread and availability, as of Nov 2026

### Takeaway
Large within-family spreads exist: Claude Haiku 5.5 vs Sonnet 5.5 is 20x (prompts up to 100k tokens), GPT-5.4-nano vs GPT-5.4 is about 12x, GPT-5.6-luna vs sol is 20x input / 16.7x output, and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite vs 3.1 Pro is 8x. Open-weight families are flatter within a vendor (gpt-oss 120b vs 20b is only 2x on Groq), so for 10x+ routing economics pair a nano/lite/haiku tier against a flagship tier, or an open small model against a closed flagship. Free tiers (Groq, Gemini) are too throttled for repo-level work.

## 5. Rough dollar cost to build a 300 tasks x 6 arms x 3 seeds arm library

### Takeaway
5,400 runs cost on the order of $40-$140 for single-file repair and roughly $1k-$5k for repository-level agentic repair, with the strong arms dominating spend (typically more than 75% of the total). Published measured costs on SWE-bench Verified (SWE-Router) are $0.033-$0.045 per instance for cheap models and $0.78 for gemini-3-pro, which scales to about $30-$41 per cheap arm and about $700 per strong arm for 900 runs.

### Cited Findings
- Measured repository-level cost per instance (SWE-Router, mini-SWE-agent, 75-step budget, 500 SWE-bench Verified instances, one trajectory each): gpt-5-mini $16.6 total = $0.033 per instance; deepseek-v3.2 $22.6 = $0.045; gemini-3-pro-preview $391.3 = $0.78. Whole-study totals for all splits $105.4, $130.5 and $608.2. — [SWE-Router html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053)
- mini-SWE-agent: "around 30ct per instance at maximum performance" and "agents spend most of their money on instances they cannot solve", with gains marginal beyond about a 50-step limit (SWE-bench blog post dated 2025-08-19, older models). — [SWE-bench blog](https://www.swebench.com/post-250820-mini-roulette.html)
- Agentless abstract cites "low cost ($0.70)" with 32.00% (96 fixes) on SWE-bench Lite; the unit (per issue) is not stated on the abstract page (ABSTRACT only). — [Agentless abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01489)
- Single-file generation length: in Resample-or-Reroute's BigCodeBench bank, the average was 339.710 generated tokens per draw for reroute (Qwen 14B) vs 509.705 for resample (generated tokens only; prompt tokens not stated). — [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08665)
- A web-search summary (not verified; the source PDF exceeded the fetch size limit) attributed to arXiv 2606.14061 a figure of 639,474 input tokens and 9,775 output tokens per instance (about $0.12) for GPT-5-mini on 500 SWE-bench Verified instances with a non-mini-SWE-agent scaffold. Treat as unverified. — [arXiv 2606.14061 (not read)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14061)

**Scenario arithmetic (my calculation; 900 runs = 300 tasks x 3 seeds per arm; list prices from section 4; no caching, batch, or retry/failed-run overhead)**
Arms used: gpt-oss-20b (Groq $0.075 / $0.30), gpt-oss-120b (Groq $0.15 / $0.60), DeepSeek flash (off-peak cache-miss $0.15 / $0.60), gpt-5.4-mini ($0.75 / $4.50), Claude Sonnet 5.5 ($2 / $10), gpt-5.5 ($5 / $30).
- Single-file, ASSUMED 2k input / 0.6k output tokens per run: per-run costs $0.0003, $0.0007, $0.0007, $0.0042, $0.0100, $0.0280; 5,400-run total about $39 (gpt-5.5 about $25 of it).
- Single-file with reasoning, ASSUMED 2k input / 3k output: about $139 total (gpt-5.5 about $90).
- Repo-level, ASSUMED 100k input / 5k output (cache-heavy agent): about $938 total (gpt-5.5 about $585, Sonnet 5.5 about $225).
- Repo-level, 639k input / 9.8k output (the unverified figure above, no cache discount): about $5,081 total (gpt-5.5 about $3,142, Sonnet 5.5 about $1,239).
- Using SWE-Router's measured per-instance costs directly: 900 runs = about $30 (gpt-5-mini), about $41 (deepseek-v3.2), about $704 (gemini-3-pro). Three cheap arms (about $0.04 each) plus three gemini-3-pro-class strong arms would be roughly 3 x $36 + 3 x $704 = about $2.2k.

### Inferences
- The cost-quality frontier a routing study needs is dominated by the strong arms; spending on cheap arms is almost free, so adding more cheap arms/seeds is the cheap way to raise power on the router side, while strong-arm seeds are the budget driver. A 1-seed strong arm plus 3-seed cheap arms would cut the 3-strong-arm repo-level total by about two thirds.
- Realized cost depends on agent step budget, caching, and failed-run overhead; the published cache-adjusted per-instance costs ($0.03-$0.05 for cheap models) are well below uncached token arithmetic ($0.12-$0.5 for similar token counts at current list prices), so budget a 2-5x contingency until pilot runs measure tokens and cache hit rates.
- Single-file repair (HumanEval/MBPP/BigCodeBench-style) is cheap enough that an entire 5,400-run library fits well inside a few hundred dollars even with reasoning models.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or directly verified per-task token counts (input/output) for mini-SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified were found; the 639k/9.8k figure is secondhand and unverified, and Agentless's $0.70 unit is unconfirmed.
- Single-file repair token budgets (prompt length, reasoning-token inflation) are my assumptions; no paper in this set reports prompt tokens for single-file repair tasks.
- Cost of failed runs, retries, evaluation harness compute (Docker containers for SWE-bench) and verifier calls were not estimated.
